# Theravada room: reference

Room id: `theravada`
Chosen temple: **Wat Phra Si Rattana Mahathat Woramahawihan ("Wat Yai"), Phitsanulok, Thailand.**
Main image: **Phra Phuttha Chinnarat (Phra Buddha Chinnarat / Jinaraja)** in the main western viharn.

Anything marked **(uncertain)** is an estimate, comes from only one weak source, or was inferred from photos rather than taken from a published measurement.

**Decision: the Phra Buddha Chinnarat at Wat Phra Si Rattana Mahathat.** It wins criterion 1, where Wat Phra Kaew loses because of the photo ban, and it wins criterion 2, where Wat Phra Kaew loses on image size and Wat Pho on the reclining posture. It is among the best-known images in Thai Buddhism, which covers criterion 3.

**Note on the ubosot request.** At Wat Yai the Chinnarat is housed in a **viharn**, not the ubosot, so the real hall has **no sema stones** around it. Wat Yai has a separate ubosot that does have sema. One real combination does put a Chinnarat image inside a sema-marked ubosot: **Wat Benchamabophit**, whose ubosot holds the 1901 royal replica with 8 sema. A build that places the Chinnarat in a hall ringed by 8 sema therefore has a real precedent. Treat it as a deliberate, documented blend: the Wat Yai image and interior, plus central-Thai ubosot features such as sema and heavier glass mosaic taken from Bangkok royal temples. Section 9 says which is which.

## 1. Identity

- **Temple, English:** Wat Phra Si Rattana Mahathat Woramahawihan ("Temple of the Great Jewelled Relic"). Locals call it **Wat Yai** ("Big Temple") or Wat Phra Si.
- **Temple, Thai:** วัดพระศรีรัตนมหาธาตุวรมหาวิหาร. Colloquially วัดใหญ่.
- **Image, Thai:** พระพุทธชินราช (Phra Phuttha Chinnarat). Locals often say หลวงพ่อพระพุทธชินราช ("Luang Pho").
- **Tibetan:** there is no established Tibetan name. A literal rendering of *Jinarāja* ("King of Victors") would be **སངས་རྒྱས་རྒྱལ་བའི་རྒྱལ་པོ** (sangs rgyas rgyal ba'i rgyal po). This is a **(uncertain) proposed translation** and should be reviewed by a Tibetan editor.
- **Location:** east bank of the Nan River, central Phitsanulok, beside Naresuan Bridge and opposite the Provincial Hall. About 337 km north of Bangkok. Coordinates are approximately 16.8236 N, 100.2622 E. The grounds cover about 36 rai (about 5.8 ha).
- **Tradition:** Theravada. It is a royal first-class temple (*Woramahawihan*).
- **History:**
  - Founded around 1357 CE (BE 1900) under the Sukhothai king Maha Thammaracha I (Li Thai). Some sources give the founding to "King Lithai" and some to Maha Thammaracha I; these are usually the same person.
  - Three bronze images were cast together: Phra Chinnarat, Phra Chinnasi and Phra Si Satsada. Tradition says the Chinnarat needed three pourings.
  - The image was first gilded in 1603 (BE 2146) under King Ekathotsarot of Ayutthaya.
  - King Borommakot donated the mother-of-pearl doors in 1756.
  - King Chulalongkorn (Rama V) took Phra Chinnasi and Phra Si Satsada to Wat Bowonniwet in Bangkok. In 1901 he regilded the Chinnarat and had a replica cast for Wat Benchamabophit.
  - The image was regilded again in 2004 under King Bhumibol.
- **Why this temple:** see section 0. In short: photography is allowed, the interior is heavily covered on Commons (PD and CC BY / BY-SA), and many videos exist. The image is the most famous and most-copied Buddha image in Thailand, and its silhouette (flame aureole plus naga arch) is unique.

## 2. Main statue: Phra Buddha Chinnarat

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Posture | Seated in the Sukhothai manner (*virasana*, half-lotus): right leg over left, right sole turned up. **Maravijaya** ("victory over Mara"), the earth-touching gesture. |
| Mudra, exact | **Right hand** rests palm-down over the right knee, fingers straight and pointing down so the fingertips reach toward the earth or the top of the base. **Left hand** lies palm-up in the lap, centred below the navel. |
| Signature hand feature | **All four fingers of each hand are the same length** (Thai: *thikhangkhuli*). The tips line up in a straight edge. It is listed by every Thai source and is visible in close-ups. |
| Size | Lap width 5 sok 1 khuep 5 niu, about **2.875 m** (1 sok = 50 cm, 1 khuep = 25 cm, 1 niu = 2.45 cm). Height is given as 7 sok (about **3.5 m**); other sources give 3.72 m or 3.75 m. Use **3.7 m** from the base of the legs to the tip of the flame. That is about 2.1 times a standing person, and a kneeling devotee (about 1.1 m eye height) looks up at it. |
| Base and throne | It sits on a high gilded pedestal (*chukchi*). From photos the pedestal is about 1.5–2 m tall and stepped, with mouldings and glass inlay **(uncertain)**. Altogether the image's lap sits at about **2 m** above the floor and the flame tip at about **5.5–6 m (uncertain)**. Offering tables, candle stands and smaller images crowd the front of the pedestal. |
| Material and surface | **Bronze**, lacquered and gilded. It does not look like matte stacked gold leaf: the surface reads as **smooth, mirror-like, warm gold** with soft specular highlights. Use metalness 1.0, roughness 0.25–0.35. |
| Face | Round, full face (rounder than the classic "bael-fruit" oval of Sukhothai). High **arched eyebrows** that join over the nose. Downcast eyes, half-closed and looking toward the kneeling devotee. Small serene smile. Long earlobes with flared tips. A small urna is noted by Thai Wikipedia but is not prominent. |
| Hair and ushnisha | Large **snail-shell curls**. A low ushnisha topped by a **tall flame finial (*ketumala / rasmi*)**, about 0.6–0.8 m **(uncertain)**. This is a classic Sukhothai feature. |
| Robe | Worn **over the left shoulder, with the right shoulder and arm bare**. The *sanghati* flap falls from the left shoulder to near the navel, and its end is cut into a **"centipede-fang"** serrated tip inlaid with glass. The robe is gilded like the body; its edges show only as slight raised lines. Devotees do not normally drape a cloth on the main image. |
| Ornaments | None in everyday view. Royal offerings such as Rama V's diamond sash (1901) and royal orders are kept in the museum or worn only on ceremonial occasions **(uncertain how often)**. |
| **Aureole (*sum ruean kaeo*)** | Carved **teak, black-lacquered and gilded**. Made separately from the image and later, generally dated to the **Ayutthaya period**. It is a pointed arch that frames the whole seated body, not only the head. The arch is formed by **two naga bodies** rising from both sides of the base. Along its outer edge runs a row of **flame-like kranok tongues**. **Makara figures disgorging the nagas** sit at the lower ends. Thai press describes a makara with an elephant-like trunk at the tips and *hera* (crocodile-dragon) figures along the arch. Exact motif placement is **(uncertain)**; check it against Commons close-ups. The arch rises to about 1.5–2 m above the flame finial **(uncertain)**. **Inside the arch is a dark (black or deep red lacquer) field**, and the golden body stands out against it. This dark field is key to legibility. |
| Guardians | Two small **bronze guardian figures** at the image's knees, probably Ayutthaya period: **Thao Wessuwan** (Vaisravana) at the **left knee** and the yaksha **Alavaka** at the **right knee**, both from the image's own point of view. Each is about 0.6–1 m **(uncertain)**. |
| Surroundings | Rows of smaller gilded seated Buddha images on both sides along the front wall. Two **Ayutthaya-period wooden pulpits (*thammat*)** stand on either side of the image: the larger is for the monk chanting in Pali, the smaller for a monk who translates into Thai. In front: offering tables with brass candle stands, vases of lotus buds, flower garlands, a donation box, and a low railing or barrier keeping devotees a few metres back **(uncertain distance, about 4–6 m)**. There is no glass case. |

### Five features that make it instantly recognizable (in priority order)

1. **The flame-edged naga aureole**: a pointed, gilded arch framing the whole body, with a **dark field inside** so the gold body stands out.
2. **The glowing, smooth gold body against a dim hall**: a single luminous figure at the end of a long dark nave of black-and-gold columns.
3. **Maravijaya pose with equal-length fingers**: the right hand drops over the right knee, the left lies in the lap.
4. **The tall Sukhothai flame finial** on top of the head, with **snail-shell curls** and **high arched eyebrows**.
5. **The sanghati flap** over the left shoulder with a serrated "centipede-fang" tip, and the **two small guardians** at the knees.

## 3. Interior (main western viharn)

**Footprint.** OpenStreetMap maps the hall as about **38 m (E–W) × 10 m (N–S)** (ODbL). That outline is probably the wall line only; with the porch and the eaves that overhang the side aisles, the building looks wider. The interior has a nave with lower side aisles. Use **36 m × 14 m interior** for the build **(uncertain)**.

**Orientation.** The hall's long axis runs east–west. **The entrance is at the west end, facing the Nan River, and the image sits at the east end facing west.** On the equinoxes the setting sun enters the doors and lights the image (MCOT news, 24 Sept 2026).

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Plan | Basilica-like. A wide central nave runs between **two rows of tall columns**, with lower side aisles under the sweeping eaves. The image is at the east end. A front porch at the west end has the mother-of-pearl doors. |
| Dimensions (build values) | Interior 36 m long × 14 m wide. The nave is about 7 m wide between column rows; each side aisle is about 3.5 m. Nave ridge ceiling about **11–12 m**; aisle ceiling about **4–5 m (uncertain)**. Image base front at about 30 m from the door. |
| Columns | Commonly described as "graceful **black and gold** columns". Black lacquer (very dark, slightly green or blue black) with gilt stencil (*lai rot nam*) patterns that are dense near the top and base. Capitals are gilded **lotus-bud** clusters (*bua tom*). They taper and lean slightly inward in the Sukhothai/Ayutthaya manner. About **6–8 columns per row (uncertain)**, about 0.7 m in diameter. |
| Ceiling | Tiered and stepped to follow the roof tiers. Dark red ground with gilt star or rosette motifs (*dao phedan*) **(uncertain)**. A Commons photo is captioned "ceiling decoration". In Rama V's time glass panes (skylights) were let into the roof; before that the hall was darker (Silpakorn scholar quoted in Thai press). |
| Walls and murals | Upper walls between the windows carry **Rattanakosin-era (19th to early 20th century) murals**: scenes from the life of the Buddha and **Jataka** tales. One traveller also reports battle scenes. The palette is earthy: ochre, brick red, dull green and indigo on a cream ground, with gold accents. The lower walls are plainer **(uncertain)**. The inner faces of the doors and windows are painted or gilded; Commons has a public-domain photo of the inside of the door. |
| Doors | One pair of **mother-of-pearl inlaid doors**, each panel 1 m wide × 4.5 m high, donated in 1756 by King Borommakot (late Ayutthaya). Black lacquer ground with iridescent nacre in tight scrolling kranok and Himmaphan patterns. The original carved doors were moved to another viharn. |
| Windows | Tall, narrow windows along both side walls, with shutters painted inside **(uncertain count, about 5–7 per side)**. |
| Floor | Polished stone or tile, covered in front of the image with **red carpets or mats** where devotees kneel **(uncertain material)**. |
| Lighting | Low natural light: the front doors (a bright rectangle behind the visitor), the side windows and the roof glass. The **image is lit and glows**. Modern floodlights are likely; in photos the image is the brightest object in the frame. Candle flames burn at the front stands. **(Uncertain)**: chandeliers or hanging lamps along the nave. |
| Seating | No benches. Devotees **kneel or sit side-saddle (*phap phiab*) on the carpeted floor** of the nave, facing east. Monks chanting for the public sit on the **pulpit** or a **raised monks' platform (*asana song*) along one side wall**, near the front and facing across the hall. The exact side at this hall is **(uncertain)**; the Thai default is the image's right side. |
| Secondary statues | Rows of smaller gilded Buddha images along the east wall and at the image's sides. Images of revered monks. Commons also has photos of *thephanom* (kneeling, praying celestials) as stair or decor figures. |
| Clutter | Brass candle stands, lotus-bud vases, jasmine and marigold garlands, gold-leaf packets on a tray, donation boxes, folded offering cloths, amulet counters near the entrance (one guide notes heavy commerce inside the hall), "photograph only while seated" signs. |

## 4. Exterior and surroundings

### The viharn

- **Form:** a long gabled hall (*wihan song rong*) with a **three-tier roof** whose lower skirts **slope down to almost head height** on both sides. This low, heavy roof is the single most distinctive feature of the exterior. The roof layers telescope down toward the entrance gable.
- **Roof finials:** gilded **chofa** (bird-beak or Garuda finial) at the gable peaks. **Naga bargeboards (*lamyong*)** with fin-like **bai raka** blades. **Hang hong** (upturned naga heads) at the bottom of each roof tier.
- **Roof tiles:** glazed tiles in orange-red with a darker green or brown border **(uncertain colours)**.
- **Gable (*na ban*):** carved, gilded teak, with gold relief on a coloured ground, likely red or glass mosaic **(uncertain)**.
- **Walls:** whitewashed masonry **(uncertain)**. A front porch (*mukh*) with columns stands at the west end.
- **Style and date:** described as Sukhothai in origin, with an Ayutthaya-period (18th c.) form. One travel source calls it "Lanna style" **(questionable)**.
- **Sema:** none around the viharn (see section 0). Sema mark Wat Yai's separate ubosot.

### Compound

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Prang** | Directly **behind (east of) the viharn**, at the centre of the temple. Ayutthaya-style, Khmer-derived corncob tower about **36 m** tall. The upper section is **gilded**. It has four rows of niches holding Buddha images and an internal stair; it enshrines relics. |
| Cloister (*phra rabiang*) | Surrounds the prang. It is lined with **rows of seated and standing Buddha images** and is described as two-tiered in the original plan. |
| Other viharns | The viharn of Phra Chinnasi (north), the Phra Chao Khao Nipphan viharn (south, outside the cloister, with a casket shrine), and the Phra Lue viharn among **three bodhi trees (*Pho Sam Sao*)**, the traditional casting site. |
| Phra Attharot | East of the prang: a **standing Buddha about 9–10 m** tall, right hand raised. It stands among the **laterite-pillar ruins** of the "nine-bay viharn" (about 44 × 19 m footprint on OSM). |
| Other | Phra Phutthachinnarat National Museum (north side). Bell and drum tower (**uncertain location**). Bodhi trees. Stalls selling amulets, gold leaf and lotus. A riverside landing in front. |
| Landscape | Flat river plain. The **Nan River** lies about 150 m west. Naresuan Bridge is to the south-west. A city skyline is low; the Provincial Hall stands across the river. |

## 5. Sound and instruments

### Chant

- **Language and style:** **Pali**, in Thai pronunciation. Monks recite together in near-monotone unison, roughly within a 3–4 semitone band. Male voices sit at a fundamental of about 100–140 Hz. The rhythm is steady and syllabic: long syllables take about two beats and short ones one, at about 4–6 syllables per second. A leader intones the opening phrase alone ("*Handa mayaṃ ...*") and the group joins. Phrase ends drop slightly in pitch and are followed by short breaths. There is also a slower, melodic *sarabhañña* style for special texts.
- **Morning chanting (*tham wat chao*):**
  - *Arahaṃ sammāsambuddho* (Ratanattaya-vandanā)
  - *Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa* ×3
  - Buddhābhitthuti, Dhammābhitthuti, Saṅghābhitthuti
  - Ratanattayappaṇāmagāthā
  - Saṃvegaparikittanapāṭha
  - Taṅkhaṇikapaccavekkhaṇa (reflection on the requisites)
- **Evening chanting (*tham wat yen*):**
  - Buddhānussati and Buddhābhigīti
  - Dhammānussati and Dhammābhigīti
  - Saṅghānussati and Saṅghābhigīti
  - Atītapaccavekkhaṇa
- **Popular lay chants at this shrine:** *Itipiso* (the Buddha's qualities), *Jinapañjara gāthā*, *Jayamaṅgala gāthā* ("*Bāhuṃ ...*"), and a devotional *katha bucha Phra Phuttha Chinnarat* (exact text **uncertain**).
- **Where:** a recording exists of Wat Yai monks at **evening chanting in front of the Chinnarat** (46 min; see section 8). The larger pulpit is used for Pali chanting.

## 6. Rituals and gestures

### Arrival and greeting
- Shoes off at the threshold. Shoulders and knees covered; wraps are available at the gate.
- Enter quietly and kneel facing the image.

### Five-point bow (*benjangkapradit*), performed three times

The three bows are for the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Sangha.

1. Kneel facing the image.
   - Men: **toes tucked under, sitting back on the heels**.
   - Women: commonly taught with the **tops of the feet flat** (soles up), sitting on the heels.
   - The male/female difference is commonly taught but only weakly sourced **(uncertain)**.
2. Join the palms in **añjali (wai)** at the chest.
3. Raise the palms to the forehead, with the thumbs touching between the eyebrows.
4. Bend forward and lower the **forearms and palms flat on the floor**, palms down and about a forehead-width apart, elbows close to the knees.
5. Touch the **forehead to the floor** between the hands. The five points are both knees, both hands (with forearms) and the forehead.
6. Rise back to the wai at the chest.
7. Repeat steps 3–6 **three times**, then finish with a wai.

Timing for animation: about 2.5–3 s per bow, about 10 s for the whole sequence.

### Sitting
- After bowing, or while listening to chanting, sit **side-saddle (*phap phiab*)**: legs folded to one side, **feet pointing backward and away from the image**.
- Never point your soles at the Buddha or at monks, and never stretch your legs toward them.
- Inside this hall, people **photograph only while seated or kneeling**. Standing is forbidden in front of the image (a rule posted in the hall).

### Offerings
- **Lotus buds** (*dok bua tum*), often with the outer petals folded back, placed in vases before the image.
- **Three incense sticks** (one per Triple Gem) and **one or two candles**, usually yellow.
- **Jasmine and marigold garlands** (*phuang malai*).
- **Gold leaf**: small squares on tissue (*pid thong*), applied to secondary images or a designated image within reach. The main image is out of reach on its base **(uncertain where gold leaf is applied at Wat Yai)**.
- Coins and notes in donation boxes.
- Incense is often burned in a large sand-filled urn outside the door **(uncertain)**.

### Circumambulation
- **Clockwise** (keeping the shrine on your right), three rounds (*wian tian*). This is done around the prang and cloister on Visakha Bucha, Makha Bucha and Asanha Bucha nights, carrying a candle, incense and a lotus.

**Lighting**

- **Day:**
  - Exterior: harsh tropical sun, about 100k lux; a low-contrast hazy sky (`#CFE0EA`).
  - Interior: dim, about 1–2 stops darker than the porch. Light comes from the bright west doorway, which backlights visitors, plus thin window shafts and roof-glass glow on the upper nave.
  - The image is the brightest element. Use a warm key spot from above-front, a weak fill, and emissive bloom on the gold.
  - The dark aureole field and black columns make the contrast.
- **Late afternoon:** low western sun pours down the nave through the doors. Near the equinoxes it reaches the image.
- **Night:**
  - Exterior: the prang and viharn are floodlit gold and white; dark sky.
  - Interior: warm lamps, candle points at the front, and the image spot-lit.
  - Overall colour temperature about 2700–3200 K.

### Must-have
1. The Chinnarat: gilded, metallic, smooth. Maravijaya hands with equal fingers, flame finial, sanghati flap.
2. The **naga-flame aureole** with a dark inner field.
3. A dim nave with **two rows of black-and-gold lotus-capital columns** leading to the image.
4. Lighting: a spot on the image, a bright west doorway behind, candles at the front, gold bloom.
5. Kneeling area plus the **five-point bow ×3** emote, and *phap phiab* sitting with feet pointing away from the image.
6. Exterior: a 3-tier low-sweeping roof with chofa, naga bargeboards and hang hong; a gilded gable; white walls.
7. Sound: evening Pali chant loop, temple bell (three-stroke close), wind bells in the eaves.

## Summary (10 lines)

1. Chosen temple: Wat Phra Si Rattana Mahathat ("Wat Yai"), Phitsanulok. Main image: Phra Buddha Chinnarat, in the west-facing main viharn.
2. Why: interior photography is allowed (seated or kneeling only), about 75 Commons files are PD, CC BY or CC BY-SA, and a 46-minute recording of evening chanting in front of the image exists. Wat Phra Kaew bans interior photos; Wat Pho's image is reclining.
3. Feature 1: a flame-edged gilded naga arch aureole with a dark inner field, framing the whole body.
4. Feature 2: a mirror-smooth gilded bronze body (about 3.7 m, lap 2.875 m) glowing at the end of a dim nave of black-and-gold columns.
5. Feature 3: Maravijaya hands with all four fingers equal in length (right hand over the right knee, left in the lap).
6. Feature 4: a tall Sukhothai flame finial, snail-shell curls, high arched eyebrows and a round face.
7. Feature 5: the sanghati flap with a serrated "centipede-fang" tip, and the small bronze guardians Thao Wessuwan and Alavaka at the knees.
8. Must-build exterior cue: a three-tier roof sweeping down to almost head height, with chofa, naga bargeboards, a gilded gable, and the gilded prang behind. Sound: Pali chant, bell, drum, gong, eave wind bells.
9. Open questions:
   - The hall's true interior width, column count and ceiling height. OSM gives a 38 × 10 m footprint.
   - The exact motif layout of the aureole.
   - The exact side and height of the monks' platform.
   - Daily chanting times at this temple.
   - Where devotees apply gold leaf.
10. Design decision needed: the real Chinnarat hall is a viharn without sema. Either add 8 sema as a documented Benchamabophit-style ubosot blend, or keep it accurate and put sema on a small separate ubosot in the compound.
