# Tibetan (Vajrayana) room: reference

Room id: `tibetan`. Chosen temple: **the Golden Temple (Padmasambhava Buddhist Vihara) of
Namdroling Monastery, Bylakuppe, Karnataka, India.**

Research date: 2026-11-03. Sources are linked inline and in section 8. Estimates are
labelled; anything marked **(uncertain)** should be checked against photos before it
drives a build decision. Tibetan spellings should go to a native speaker for review.

**Why Namdroling.** It is the only candidate that scores well on all three criteria in
order. The interior and statues are among the most photographed of any Tibetan temple
(free licences on Commons, see section 8). The main image group is huge, symmetric,
brightly gilded and visible from the doorway. Tibetans, especially in exile, know it well
as the seat of Penor Rinpoche and the Palyul Nyingma lineage in India. Bonus for the game:
its three figures are Shakyamuni, Guru Rinpoche and Amitayus, an instantly "Tibetan"
trio that no Theravada or East Asian room will duplicate.

**History.**
- **1963:** Penor Rinpoche (Pema Norbu), 11th throne-holder of the Palyul lineage, founds
  Namdroling in exile with about ten monks in a bamboo temple of roughly 7 m²
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namdroling_Monastery); founder's account
  quoted in the [Pema video description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PI-4Sy0N0fA)).
- **1978:** the Ngagyur Nyingma Institute (shedra) opens.
- **1993:** the Tsogyal Shedrub Dargye Ling nunnery opens.
- **24 September 1999:** the Golden Temple (Padmasambhava Buddhist Vihara) is inaugurated.
- **13 December 2004:** the Zangdog Palri temple is inaugurated.
- **Today:** the monastery has more than 5,000 monks and nuns (a census counted over 4,000
  monks and 800 nuns) and 16 temples. Penor Rinpoche died in 2009.

**Why this temple.**
- Commons category "Statues of the Buddha at Namdroling Monastery" has 140 files, and
  "Interior of Padmasambhava Buddhist Vihara" has 75.
- The official monastery site gives statue heights and materials.
- Visitors may photograph when no ceremony is running. One visitor reports that the
  empty hall may be photographed after noon
  ([evolveback blog](https://www.evolveback.com/blogs/namdroling---the-refuge.html)).
- Dozens of YouTube walk-throughs, drone flights and puja recordings exist.

## 2. Main statue (most important)

The altar holds three colossal gilded figures. **The main statue is the centre one,
Buddha Shakyamuni.** The two flanking figures are part of what makes the hall
recognizable, so they are specified too.

| Viewer's position | Figure | Height (official) |
|---|---|---|
| Left | Guru Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche) | 58 ft (17.7 m) |
| **Centre** | **Buddha Shakyamuni** | **60 ft (18.3 m) "from the base"** |
| Right | Buddha Amitayus | 58 ft (17.7 m) |

All three are copper gilded with gold, and each is filled with scriptures, relics,
miniature clay stupas (tsa-tsa) and small statues
([namdroling.net](https://www.namdroling.net/Portal/Page/The-Three-Great-Statues-inside-the-Golden-Temple)).
Some tourist sites say "40 ft" or "55 ft". **Use the official 60/58 ft.**

### 2a. Shakyamuni (centre)

**Name.** ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ། (Shakya Thubpa); also སྟོན་པ། (Tönpa, "the Teacher").

**Posture and mudra.**
- Seated in full vajra (lotus) posture.
- **Right hand: earth-touching (bhumisparsha).** The arm hangs over the right knee, palm
  inward, and the fingertips point down toward the lotus.
- **Left hand: meditation (dhyana).** It rests palm-up in the lap and holds a dark,
  near-black **alms bowl** (clearly visible in the photos).

**Height and proportions.** These are estimated from Commons photos with people for scale
**(uncertain, ±15%)**. The official 18.3 m is measured from the base, and it appears to
include the altar platform.

| Part of the stack | Estimated height |
|---|---|
| Altar platform and glass-case zone | about 5 m |
| Lotus and square throne | about 3 m |
| **Seated figure** | **about 7.5 m** (top of ushnisha at about 15.5 m above the floor) |
| Arched throne-back (torana) | rises to about 19 to 20 m |
| Torana width | about 11 m |

A 1.7 m adult reaches about the top of the glass display cases at the altar's foot.

**Material and surface.** Gilded copper with a smooth, mirror-like polished gold finish.
Robe folds are shallow incised ridges; it is not a hammered or textured gold-leaf look.

**Face.**
- Gold skin.
- Painted features: thin dark arched brows, white eyes with dark pupils, half-lowered
  gaze, small red lips, and a calm, slightly smiling expression.
- Long earlobes.
- No prominent urna in the photos **(uncertain; it may be a small painted dot)**.

**Hair and ushnisha.** Hair curls painted **lapis/cobalt blue**. The ushnisha is blue
with a small gold finial jewel on top.

**Robe.** Monastic robe draped over the left shoulder. The diagonal hem runs from the left
shoulder down across the chest to the right waist, so the right chest and shoulder are
essentially bare. The robe is entirely gilded, the same gold as the skin, with a subtle
raised hem border. No cloth robe is draped on it by devotees, because the statue is far
out of reach.

**Crown and ornaments.** None; it is a nirmanakaya Buddha.

**Halo and aureole.**
- **Head halo:** a circular disc in **turquoise/teal**.
- **Body aureole:** an arched niche in **deep royal blue** behind the torso.
- **Torana:** both sit inside a tall gilded arch with a pointed, ogee-like crown. The
  frame is a gold border with jewel-coloured inlays, and its field is red and green.
- **Torana figures:** a classic Tibetan "six ornaments" (gyen drug) throne-back, with
  - a gold **garuda (khyung)** at the apex, holding green nagas;
  - **makara** sea monsters curling down the sides;
  - small gilded riders, lions and elephants at crossbar level;
  - a horizontal gilded crossbar at shoulder height whose ends project like brackets.

**Throne and pedestal.**
- A double row of gilded lotus petals.
- Below it, a rectangular gilded throne with a frieze and garlands of real flowers.
- Below that, the long altar platform. Its front band is **dark blue with gold square
  medallions**. Under the band is a row of **glass-fronted display cases full of small
  gilded statues**, and a glass and steel rail at about 1.1 m.

**What surrounds it.**
- **Between the three statues:** tall pillars, red and teal, with **gilded and green
  dragons coiling up them**.
- **Outermost pillars:** wrapped in horizontal **multicoloured brocade stripes**.
- **Back wall:** a mural of **blue sky with swirling white, pink and green clouds** and
  small deity figures.
- **Above:** clusters of **crystal chandeliers** hang in front of the Buddha's face.
- **At the altar's foot, on the axis:** a gilded **throne** holding a framed **portrait
  of Penor Rinpoche**, a second smaller portrait throne beside it, offering tables,
  flower vases, khatas and electric lamps.

### 2b. Guru Padmasambhava (viewer's left)

**Name.** གུ་རུ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། / པདྨ་འབྱུང་གནས། (Guru Rinpoche / Pema Jungne).

**Posture.** Seated, in royal ease or a semi-relaxed posture **(uncertain; photos show
both knees spread wide on the lotus)**.

**Right hand.** On the right knee, holding a gold **vajra** in the threatening/teaching
gesture **(standard iconography; not clearly visible in the reviewed photos)**.

**Left hand.** In the lap, holding a **skull cup (kapala)** with a small **long-life vase**
standing in it.

**Khatvanga.** The trident staff is held in the crook of the left arm. It rises past the
shoulder and carries a trident, stacked heads, a double vajra, and red and white ribbons.

**Head and face.**
- The distinctive **lotus hat (pema shamo)**: gold, tall, with ear flaps, and topped by a
  half-vajra and a **vulture feather**.
- **Wide-open, staring, painted eyes**, a **thin moustache** and a small goatee; a
  slightly fierce, smiling expression.

**Body and ornaments.** Layered robes (inner robe, Dharma robe, brocade cloak), all
gilded. Large round earrings and a jewelled necklace inlaid in blue and red.

**Halo.**
- A circular halo with a teal/blue centre and a broad gilded rim, ringed with blue, red
  and green "jewels".
- An outer arched aureole in the same style.
- A lotus throne with real flower pots.

### 2c. Amitayus (viewer's right)

**Name.** ཚེ་དཔག་མེད། (Tsepakme), the Buddha of Long Life.

**Form.** Sambhogakaya (crowned bodhisattva) form, seated in vajra posture. **Both hands
in dhyana mudra** in the lap, cradling a **long-life vase (tsebum)**. The fingertips just
touch.

**Crown and ornaments.**
- A tall **five-leaf gold crown** with a blue topknot and a jewel finial.
- Large round disc earrings, blue with a red centre.
- Several jewelled necklaces with blue and red stones.

**Face.** Gold, with painted blue-black brows and eyes and red lips.

**Skin and halo.** The skin is **gilded, not red** (Amitayus is red in thangkas, but here
he is gold). The halo matches Padmasambhava's.

### 2d. The 5 features that make it instantly recognizable (priority order)

1. **Three colossal gilded seated figures side by side across the whole back wall.** They
   are nearly the same height: centre Buddha 60 ft, flanks 58 ft. The centre one is
   slightly taller and framed by a pointed torana. The two flanks sit inside **large
   circular halos**.
2. **Shakyamuni's earth-touching right hand and the dark alms bowl in his left hand**,
   with **blue hair** against a **turquoise head-halo** and a **deep-blue arched niche**.
3. **Silhouettes of the flanking figures:** **Guru Rinpoche's lotus hat with a feather and
   the trident staff** on the left; **Amitayus's tall five-point crown and hands joined
   in the lap** on the right.
4. **A dense blue-sky mural behind the statues** (blue and turquoise with white and pink
   clouds), plus **red pillars with coiling gilded dragons** between the figures and
   **rainbow-striped brocade pillars** at the ends.
5. **Altar-platform layering:** the statues stand high above head height on a long
   dark-blue and gold band. Under it are glass cases of small gold statues and a central
   lama throne with a portrait. A **mirror-polished floor** reflects everything.

## 3. Interior

All dimensions are estimates unless cited.
- The building footprint is about **42 m × 48 m**, from
  [OSM way 389045744](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/389045744) (tagged
  `building=temple`, "Namdroling Monastery"), which matches the satellite outline.
- One visitor calls the hall "about as tall as it is wide", with room for more than a
  thousand monks ([evolveback](https://www.evolveback.com/blogs/namdroling---the-refuge.html)).
- Wikipedia says the hall can seat "several thousand monks".

**Layout**, along the axis from the door (z = 0) to the back wall:

- **Entrance porch:** about 5 m deep. A row of **massive red columns** (about 1.2 m
  diameter **(uncertain)**) carries a deep painted lintel with **dragon and cloud
  friezes** (green, blue, red, gold). There are gilded door knobs and beaded or
  brocade door curtains.
- **Main hall**, about **36 m wide × 38 to 40 m deep**:
  - **Central nave:** about 26 m wide. Clear height about **22 to 25 m** to a dark,
    painted ceiling grid. 2009 photos show an exposed white-panelled steel roof truss,
    which may now be painted or boarded over **(uncertain)**.
  - **Side aisles:** about 5 m wide each. Ground level has red columns, mural panels and
    doors. Above is a **gallery or upper storey with tall glazed clerestory windows**, in
    yellow-ochre frames with blue trim, that bring in daylight.
- **Altar zone:** the last 6 to 7 m of the hall, spanning nearly its full width (about
  34 m). See section 2 for its stacking.
- **Statue x positions:** Buddha at x = 0, Padmasambhava at x ≈ −11.5 m, Amitayus at
  x ≈ +11.5 m.

**Pillars.**
- **Nave pillars:** two rows on the long axis, about 6 to 8 pillars a side
  **(uncertain)**.
  - **Red** (`#B3261E`) shafts with ornate painted capitals and brackets in green, blue
    and gold.
  - Lower halves are often wrapped in **brocade pillar-hangings (ka-phen)** in
    rainbow-striped silk.
  - Near the altar, whole shafts are wrapped in **horizontal bands of red, green, yellow,
    blue and patterned brocade**.
- **Pillars between the statues:** **gilded dragons**, gold and green, coil up them.

**Ceiling.**
- A dark ceiling grid painted with geometric and mandala motifs **(uncertain detail)**.
- A **pleated brocade valance (shambu)** runs along the top of the altar wall, in stacked
  stripes of red, green, yellow, white and blue.
- **Crystal chandeliers** hang in front of the statues.
- Long **ribbon swags** in the five colours (blue, white, red, green, yellow) are strung
  across the hall at upper level.

**Walls and murals.**
- **Altar wall:** a bright blue sky with clouds.
- **Side walls:** large painted panels of the **Nyingma refuge field and lineage
  masters**, **Guru Rinpoche's eight manifestations and his Copper-Coloured Mountain**,
  and **wrathful tantric deities, including deities in union**
  ([evolveback](https://www.evolveback.com/blogs/namdroling---the-refuge.html)).
- Murals are painted in the standard Tibetan palette: mineral blue, malachite green,
  vermilion, ochre and gold, with dark-red borders.
- Gilded **lantsa-script mantra panels** read "OM AH HUNG VAJRA GURU PADMA SIDDHI HUNG"
  ([Commons, Mikhail Esteves](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mikhail_Esteves,_Interior_of_Padmasambhava_Buddhist_Vihara.jpg)).

**Floor.**
- **Mirror-polished dark stone** (granite or marble) with **lighter cream inlaid stripes**
  forming a large rectangular grid along the aisle.
- Very reflective; the statues reflect in it in every photo.

**Lighting.**
- **Day:** daylight comes from the clerestory windows and the open doors. The statues are
  additionally lit by **bright floodlights and spotlights**, so they read warm gold
  against the blue wall.
- **Night:** crystal chandeliers, fluorescent or LED fittings in the side aisles, and
  floodlights on the statues.
- **Butter lamps** are mostly kept in a separate **Butter Lamp House**: rows of brass
  lamps on stepped shelves behind glass, near the temple
  ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ramesh_Meda,_Butter_Lamp_House_at_Namdroling_Monastery.jpg)).
- On the altar itself, a few brass butter lamps and electric lamps.

**Seating, the monk rows (the classic Tibetan dukhang layout).**
- **Long, low, continuous benches** (about 15 to 25 cm high, 0.7 to 0.8 m deep) run
  **parallel to the main axis**, from near the door to near the altar, on both sides of a
  **central aisle about 4 to 5 m wide**.
- Monks sit cross-legged **facing the aisle**, so the two sides face each other, not the
  altar.
- Each bench has a **Tibetan rug or cushion runner** (red, blue, yellow and orange with
  dragon and floral borders).
- In front of each monk is a **low wooden table (choktse)** for the loose-leaf text
  (pecha), a bell and dorje, and a tea cup.
- When the hall is empty, monks' maroon zen shawls lie folded on their places.
- **Row ends near the altar:** high seats and thrones for the **chant master (umdze)**,
  tulkus and khenpos. Sometimes a long covered table holds ritual offerings, tormas and
  flowers in the central aisle.
- **Big nga drums** with **green heads** stand on frames at the ends of rows.
- **Visitors** stand or sit at the back and along the side aisles, and are kept out of
  the monks' rows during ceremonies.

**Banners, thangkas and other furnishings.**
- **Banners:** **cylindrical silk victory banners (gyaltsen, རྒྱལ་མཚན།)** and **pillar
  banners** hang from the ceiling and pillars, in tiers of coloured silk with fringed
  flounces. Five-colour silk **phen** streamers hang along the walls.
- **Thangkas:** large thangkas in brocade mounts hang in the side aisles. Huge appliqué
  thangkas are shown only at festivals.
- **Secondary statues:** hundreds of small gilded statues fill the glass cases under the
  altar. Smaller shrines and stupas stand in the side aisles **(uncertain)**.
- **Offering tables:**
  - rows of **seven silver or brass water bowls (yönchap)**;
  - **butter lamps**;
  - **tormas** (sculpted barley-dough offerings, red and white);
  - fruit, biscuit packets, flowers in vases, and stacks of **white khatas**;
  - **donation boxes**.
- **Typical clutter:**
  - glass display cases;
  - **wall-mounted loudspeakers**;
  - **ladders** left beside the statues for cleaning;
  - a "remove shoes / silence" sign;
  - shoe racks outside;
  - folded robes on benches;
  - thermos flasks and tea cups;
  - piles of pecha texts wrapped in yellow and red cloth.

## 4. Exterior and surroundings

**Architecture.** A modern (1999) reinforced-concrete temple in **Tibetan-Chinese hybrid
style**.

**Lower block.** A broad rectangular building, two to three storeys of **cream / pale
yellow walls** (`#EDE2C0`).
- **Windows:** Tibetan **trapezoidal windows** with **black or dark-brown frames** and
  maroon lintels.
- **Parapet:** a deep **maroon-brown band** (`#5A2A26`, imitating the pembe brushwood
  frieze) studded with **gold round mirror-medallions**.
- **Plinth:** a white base.

**Entrance front.** A projecting porch with large red columns, plus a **blue band with gold
ornaments and Tibetan text** above the doors.

**Central tower over the hall.** The signature silhouette.
- Blue-painted walls (`#2F5DA8`) with ornate window bays.
- A **large framed portrait** on the front (a lama, reportedly Penor Rinpoche
  **(uncertain)**; for the game, use a thangka or the Dharma wheel instead).
- **Two or three tiers of golden, Chinese-style hipped roofs with upturned eaves**.
- A **huge gilded Dharma-wheel ring** (about 8 to 10 m in diameter **(uncertain)**)
  standing vertically on top. It frames a small golden roofed pavilion and two deer.

**Overall height.** About 35 to 40 m to the top of the wheel **(uncertain, estimated from
photos)**.

**Side wings.** Lower one-storey wings with golden roofs. **Gilded cylindrical victory
banners (gyaltsen)** and bell-shaped finials (ganjira) stand on the roof corners.

**Approach.**
- A **straight paved axial path**, about 5 to 6 m wide and about 60 to 80 m long
  **(uncertain)**.
- Low **railings** both sides: golden-yellow lattice panels on a red base, about 1 m.
- **Lamp posts** about 4 m tall.
- Clipped hedges and **lawns** on both sides.

**Trees and landscape.**
- **Tall columnar Araucaria (Cook / Norfolk pine), about 25 to 30 m**, flank the temple
  front; they are very recognizable in photos.
- Coconut and areca palms, flowering shrubs (bougainvillea, cannas), and a **round
  ornamental pond with a stone rim** beside the temple
  ([Commons side view](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Namdroling_Monastery_Golden_Temple_in_2025_-_Side_view.jpg)).
- Flat to gently rolling South-Indian farmland, coffee and areca country.
- A blue sky with big cumulus clouds in the monsoon months.

**Gates.** The **main monastery gate** is a free-standing Tibetan gateway.
- Four red-and-blue pillars painted with flower patterns, a central arch and two side
  passages.
- A **golden roof with three gilded finials** and **makara / dragon heads** on the
  eave corners.
- A blue panel with a gold Tibetan inscription, and side plaques reading "NYINGMAPA" and
  "MONASTERY".
- See [Commons entrance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Namdroling_Monastery_Golden_Temple_Entrance.jpg).

**Other buildings nearby.**
- **Prayer wheels:** an open **arcade of large prayer wheels**. Each wheel is about 1 m in
  diameter and 1.5 m tall, painted maroon and gold with blue and yellow mantra bands, on
  red iron spindles. They sit in a cream colonnade
  ([Commons, CC0](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Isabelnic_photography,_Prayer_Wheels_in_Namdroling_Monastery.jpg)).
- **Big Bell pavilion:** marked "Big Bell" on maps, east of the temple. Its bell is rung
  at sunrise.
- **Zangdog Palri temple** (2004), north-east: Guru Rinpoche's Copper-Coloured Mountain
  palace, with gold roofs.
- **Tara temple**, north.
- **Butter Lamp House.**
- **Palyul Monastery replica**, a **stupa** reliquary, monks' quarters with red tiled
  roofs, a guesthouse by the front gate, a café and souvenir shops.
- **Prayer flags (lungta):** strings in five colours at the gate and between trees, plus
  vertical **darchor** poles.

## 5. Sound and instruments

### 5a. Chant at Namdroling (Nyingma, Palyul)

**Language and style.** Liturgy in classical Tibetan (Chöke), chanted in unison.
- A deep male drone, with a **rhythmic recitation register (don)** for long texts and a
  slower **melodic register (yang, དབྱངས།)** for invocations.
- Led by the umdze's solo opening phrase, then joined by the assembly.
- Hundreds of voices in the hall give a thick, chorused, reverberant sound. The hall has
  noted acoustics
  ([evolveback](https://www.evolveback.com/blogs/namdroling---the-refuge.html)).

**Texts most likely heard:**
- **Refuge and bodhicitta**, སངས་རྒྱས་ཆོས་དང་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་མཆོག་རྣམས་ལ། (*sangye chö dang tsok kyi
  chok nam la…*): recited at the start of every session.
- **Seven-Line Prayer to Guru Rinpoche** (ཚིག་བདུན་གསོལ་འདེབས།, *Hung, Orgyen yul gyi nub
  jang tsam…*): the signature Nyingma invocation.
- **Vajra Guru mantra:** *Om Ah Hung Vajra Guru Padma Siddhi Hung*. It is inscribed in
  the hall and chanted in long repetitions.
- **Mani mantra:** *Om Mani Padme Hum* (ཨོཾ་མ་ཎི་པདྨེ་ཧཱུྃ།), Avalokiteshvara's mantra.
  Devotees and lay visitors murmur it constantly, turning wheels and counting malas.
- **Shakyamuni mantra:** *Tayata Om Muni Muni Maha Muniye Soha*.
- **Amitayus long-life mantra:** *Om Amarani Jiwantiye Soha*.
- **Palyul Namchö** ("Sky Dharma") terma liturgies, Tara praises (Dolma), and Mahakala
  and other protector pujas (videos in section 8).

### 5b. Deep "one-voice chord" overtone chant (Gyuto / Gyume)

This style, *gyüke* (རྒྱུད་སྐད།), is a speciality of the **Gelug tantric colleges** Gyume
and Gyuto, not of Nyingma Namdroling. The game's Tibetan room can still use it as its
signature chant layer, for two reasons:
- **Gyudmed (Gyume) Tantric Monastery** is in Hunsur, Karnataka, roughly 25 to 35 km
  from Bylakuppe **(uncertain distance)**. It is the same Tibetan settlement region of
  South India.
- Audiences recognize it as "Tibetan chant".

**How it works.**
- **Fundamental:** very low, about **60 to 75 Hz** (around C2 to D2), and sometimes lower
  **(estimate)**.
- **Formant:** the vocal tract is shaped so that one high harmonic is strongly reinforced.
  For low chanters the formants lock onto **harmonics 5 and 9**: the 5th is two octaves
  and a major third above the fundamental. The result is a single singer sounding like a
  chord ([ASA 1994 abstract](https://auditory.org/asamtgs/asa94aus/5aSP/5aSP6.html),
  which corroborates Smith, Stevens and Tomlinson, *JASA* 41, 1967).
- **Quality:** a growling, gravelly onset, very slow syllables (several seconds each), a
  steady pitch, and the whole assembly in unison.

**Synthesis.**
- Sawtooth or pulse source at about 65 Hz with slight jitter.
- Strong band-pass resonances at **5 × f0 (about 325 Hz)** and **9 × f0 (about
  585 Hz)**, Q about 8 to 12.
- Low-pass at about 2.5 kHz.
- 6 to 12 detuned voices (±5 to 15 cents), slow amplitude envelopes (0.5 s attack, 3 to
  6 s sustain).
- Large hall reverb, RT60 about 3 to 4 s.

**Typical puja sequence (for scripting).**
1. Before the session: **dungkar or dungchen** call from the roof, plus monks walking
   in.
2. The **umdze** intones the opening and the assembly answers in chant.
3. **Refuge and bodhicitta** prayers.
4. Main text, recited rapidly. **Nga** strokes keep the tempo; **drilbu and damaru** sound
   at mantra points.
5. **Offering section:** **gyaling** melody, then **dungchen** pair and **rolmo or
   silnyen** with an accelerating **nga** roll.
6. Long mantra repetitions.
7. Dedication prayers, a final drilbu tremolo, and silence.

**Ambient sounds of the place.**
- Hall reverb, with an RT60 of about 3 to 4 s when the hall is full.
- Bare-foot shuffle on polished stone and pages of pecha turning.
- Distant chanting from other temples, and young monks reciting texts by rote in
  courtyards (many voices at different speeds).
- Prayer-wheel squeaks and dings, pigeons and doves in the high windows (doves "flew in
  through" them per the evolveback blog), crows and mynas outside.
- Tourist murmur at the back, and monsoon rain on the roofs from June to September.
- Wind in the Araucaria pines; scooters and auto-rickshaws in the distance on Golden
  Temple Road.

## 6. Rituals and gestures

**Bow: full-length prostration** (ཕྱག་འཚལ། chaktsal; the full one is *kyangchak*),
**three times** on entering, in front of the statues:

1. Stand facing the altar, feet together.
2. Join the palms in a **"lotus bud"**: hollow between the palms, thumbs tucked inside.
3. Touch the joined hands to the **crown** (body), then the **throat** (speech), then the
   **heart** (mind). Some also touch the forehead first.
4. Drop to the knees, place the hands on the floor, and **slide forward until the whole
   body lies flat**, forehead touching, arms stretched out in front.
5. Optionally raise the joined hands above the head once.
6. Push back up to standing.

Inside crowded halls, many people do the **short (five-point) prostration** instead:
knees, palms and forehead touch the floor. Laypeople also do a simple **standing bow with
joined palms** and touch their forehead to the altar rail or a monk's text. **Avatar
animation: `tibetan-prostration`; `five-point` as an alternative.**

**Circumambulation (kora, སྐོར་ར།).** Always **clockwise**, keeping the statues and the
temple on your **right**:
- inside the hall, along the side aisles and behind or around the altar platform if it is
  open;
- outside, around the temple building and along the prayer-wheel arcade.

Prayer wheels are turned **clockwise with the right hand**
([casualwalker](https://casualwalker.com/namdroling-monastery-thegchog-namdrol-shedrub-dargyeling-the-golden-temple-in-bylakuppe-coorg-the-largest-teaching-center-of-the-nyingma-lineage-of-tibetan-buddhism-in-the-world-visit)),
while reciting *Om Mani Padme Hum* and counting on a 108-bead mala.

**Offerings.**
- **Khata (ཁ་བཏགས།):** a white silk scarf, sometimes printed with the eight auspicious
  symbols.
  1. Fold it lengthwise so the open edge faces the giver.
  2. Hold it across both palms and bow.
  3. Lay it on the altar rail, the offering table or the lama's throne, or hand it to a
     monk who places it.
- **Water bowls (ཡོན་ཆབ།, yönchap):** a straight row of **seven bowls**.
  - Filled in the **morning** (from left to right as you face the altar, a grain of rice
    apart, about a rice-grain below the brim), emptied and turned upside-down at
    **dusk**.
  - The seven offerings are drinking water, washing water, flowers, incense, light,
    perfume and food.
- **Butter lamps (མཆོད་མེ།, chöme):** lit in the **Butter Lamp House** (rows of brass
  lamps on tiered shelves). Visitors pay a small donation and monks or visitors light
  them with a long taper. A few lamps also burn on the main altar.
- **Incense:** juniper and sang smoke outside, and stick incense in a burner at the
  entrance. Smoking juniper (sang) at festivals.
- **Other offerings:** flowers, fruit, money in donation boxes, and **tormas** made by
  the monks.

**Visitor etiquette.**
- No shoes inside the hall; socks are fine.
- Keep silent. Do not point your feet at the altar, and do not walk through the monks'
  rows.
- Tourists are kept out during some ceremonies, and the hall may close from about 13:00
  to 14:00 for prayer. Photography is accepted outside ceremonies.

**Daily schedule** (time zone `Asia/Kolkata`). No official timetable was found online;
all times below are reported or **(uncertain)**.

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| About 05:00 to 05:30 | Sunrise bell and conch; **morning assembly (dawn puja)** in the temples **(uncertain)** |
| About 07:00 to 09:00 | Temple opens to visitors (sources say 07:00 or 09:00) |
| **About 13:00** | **Midday prayer** that visitors can watch, with chanting and instruments ([casualwalker](https://casualwalker.com/namdroling-monastery-thegchog-namdrol-shedrub-dargyeling-the-golden-temple-in-bylakuppe-coorg-the-largest-teaching-center-of-the-nyingma-lineage-of-tibetan-buddhism-in-the-world-visit)); horns are heard in the afternoon |
| About 18:00 | Visitors leave; **evening puja** and protector prayers **(uncertain)** |
| Until late | Students recite texts and debate into the night |

**Annual events.**
- **Losar** (Tibetan New Year, Feb/Mar).
- **Drubchen**: a week-long intensive puja around Feb/Mar
  ([2024 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=esSPYfN3tHY)).
- **Saga Dawa** (May/June).
- **Tsechu**: Guru Rinpoche days on the 10th of each lunar month, with cham dances at
  festivals.

The current TempleDef (`dawn 05:00`, `evening 18:00`) is consistent; consider adding
**`midday 13:00`**.

## 9. Build plan

### 9a. Scale

The real hall is huge (statue stack about 18 m). The visitor avatar is about 1.1 m (a
child), so **build at 0.5× real scale**:

| Element | At 0.5× |
|---|---|
| Hall | **18 m wide × 20 m deep**, nave ceiling **12 m** |
| Altar platform | **2.5 m** high |
| Buddha | seated figure **3.8 m**, top of halo arch **about 10 m** |

This keeps the avatar-to-statue ratio (about 1 : 8.5) close to an adult's ratio in the
real hall (about 1 : 9), so it still feels colossal, while staying cheap to render.
Coordinates below are **hall-local at 0.5×**: origin at the centre of the doorway
threshold, +z toward the altar, +x to the visitor's right when facing the altar, y up.

### 9c. Priorities

**Must-have:**
1. The three statues and their **five recognizability features** (section 2d), with the
   **centre Buddha spotlit**. Use a warm SpotLight from above and in front (about 30°
   down), plus warm fill. The flanking statues get a slightly lower intensity.
2. The torana and halos (teal disc, blue niche, gold frame); the dragon pillars and
   brocade pillars.
3. The blue-sky back mural and the dark-blue and gold altar band with emissive glass
   cases.
4. The reflective dark floor (`MeshStandardMaterial` with roughness 0.1; a
   reflector/SSR is optional), aisle inlay stripes, and **monk benches facing the aisle**
   with the 18 cushions.
5. Red pillars and the porch; cream exterior with maroon parapet; gold roofs and **the
   Dharma-wheel ring** on the tower.
6. Water bowls, butter lamps (flicker), khatas on the rail.
7. Sound: chant drone (gyüke-style layer plus Nyingma unison), nga, drilbu with dorje,
   rolmo, dungchen pair, damaru. `tibetan-prostration` bow.

**Should-have:**
1. Chandeliers; clerestory light shafts (additive planes); shambu valance; ceiling swags.
2. Approach path with railings, Araucaria pines, pond, gate.
3. Prayer-wheel arcade (interactive clockwise spin with a bell ding), and a clockwise
   kora path hint.
4. Gyaling, conch (dungkar) and kangling sounds; scripted puja sequence (section 5c) at
   service times; sunrise big-bell strike at the `dawn` service.
5. Side-wall mural panels (procedural or CC-licensed thangka textures).

**Nice-to-have:**
1. Butter Lamp House interior.
2. Zangdog Palri silhouette in the distance.
3. Prayer flags with wind animation.
4. Pigeons in the high windows.
5. Monsoon rain at night.
6. Festival mode (Losar banners, appliqué thangka).

## Open questions

1. **Exact interior dimensions** (hall width, depth, ceiling height) are estimates from
   the OSM footprint and photo scaling. The monastery does not publish them.
2. **Compass orientation** of the facade was not verified, so the site uses door-relative
   coordinates.
3. **The current ceiling:** an exposed steel truss (2009 photo) or a painted ceiling now?
   It is not visible in the reviewed 2017 to 2025 photos.
4. **Padmasambhava's right hand and leg posture** are not clearly visible in the reviewed
   photos; standard iconography is assumed.
5. **The portrait on the facade tower and on the altar throne:** probably Penor Rinpoche.
   Decide whether to depict a real lama's likeness in-game (a thangka is the safer
   substitute).
6. **Official daily puja times** were not found online. Only the 13:00 prayer is
   reported.
7. **Tibetan names** for the hall and "Golden Temple" need native review.
8. **Chant choice:** the deep Gyuto/Gyume overtone style is Gelug, not Namdroling's
   Nyingma liturgy. Using it as the room's chant layer is a design choice to confirm.
