# Tōdai-ji Daibutsuden (Great Buddha Hall), Nara: room reference

Room id: `mahayana`
Researched: 2026-11-03. Sources are listed at the end and cited inline as [S#]; media as [M#].
Anything marked **(uncertain)** is an estimate, comes from a single weak source, conflicts
between sources, or is general knowledge that could not be confirmed online. Check it against

## 0. Temple selection

Criteria, in order: (1) the main hall interior and main statue are well documented in photos
and video; (2) the statue is clear, central, in good view and instantly recognizable, in a
hall where devotees bow and practise; (3) fame and overall documentation.

| Candidate | (1) Interior / statue documentation | (2) Statue and practice | (3) Fame | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tōdai-ji Daibutsuden**, Nara (JP) | **Best.** The temple's own site says photos may be taken freely inside the hall; only flash, tripods, group photos and sketching are banned [S1]. Hundreds of CC-licensed interior photos on Commons, sorted by view angle [M1–M3]. Many walkthrough videos. | One colossal bronze Vairocana (14.98 m), dead centre, lit from the open front doors. **Decision: Tōdai-ji Daibutsuden.** It is the only candidate where interior photography is
officially allowed and the main image is one huge, central statue. It is also the most famous
of the six. Its weakness is that it is busier with tourists than with practitioners, and its
daily liturgy is Nara-school (Kegon) shōmyō, not the wooden-fish-paced chanting of Chinese and
Korean temples. Section 5 marks which instruments and chants are **authentic at Tōdai-ji** and
which are **pan-East-Asian layers** that the room could add on purpose (for example, a Chinese
or Korean evening chant).

## 1. Identity

- **Name.** Tōdai-ji (東大寺, "Great Eastern Temple"). Main hall: Daibutsuden (大仏殿, Great Buddha Hall), formally Kondō (金堂, Golden Hall) [S1].
  - Main image: Rushana-butsu (盧舎那仏, Vairocana Buddha), popularly *Nara no Daibutsu* [S1][S2].
  - **Tibetan:** Vairocana is རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད། (*rnam par snang mdzad*). The Avatamsaka (Kegon) Sutra is སངས་རྒྱས་ཕལ་པོ་ཆེ། (*sangs rgyas phal po che*). "Great Buddha Hall" could be rendered སངས་རྒྱས་ཆེན་པོའི་ལྷ་ཁང་། **(uncertain; needs native review)**. A phonetic for Tōdai-ji, ཐོ་དའི་ཇི།, is **(uncertain)**.
- **Location.** 406-1 Zōshi-chō, Nara, Nara Prefecture, Japan [S1]. Hall centre is about 34.6890 N, 135.8399 E (OpenStreetMap) [S19].
  - It stands at the north end of Nara Park, where wild sika deer roam the approach.
  - Time zone `Asia/Tokyo`.
- **Tradition.** Mahayana, Kegon school (華厳宗). Tōdai-ji is the head temple of the Kegon school, one of the six Nara schools. It has had strong Shingon (esoteric) ties since Kūkai [S2].
- **History.**
  - Emperor Shōmu ordered the Great Buddha in 743. Work began at Shigaraki and resumed in Nara in 745 [S2].
  - Casting took place in 747–749. The eye-opening ceremony was in 752, led by the Indian monk Bodhisena, with about 10,000 monks present [S2][S3].
  - The hall burned in 1180 and was rebuilt under Chōgen (dedicated 1195). It burned again in 1567 [S1].
  - The statue's head was recast in the Edo period (re-consecrated 1692). The present hall was finished in **1709** under the monk Kōkei. It is seven bays wide instead of the original eleven, about 30% narrower, but keeps the original height and depth [S1][S2].
  - Major restorations: 1903–1913, which added steel roof trusses, and 1974–1980 [S2][S4].
- **Why this temple.** Interior photography is officially allowed [S1]. Commons sorts the statue's photos by angle ("Front views", "Front left views", "Hands", "Head"…) [M1]. Detailed CC BY 4.0 photo series exist of the statue, its altar, both attendants and the guardians [M4–M12]. There are also a full-scale replica of a lotus petal, scale models of the hall in the hall itself, a 360° panorama set (Columbia MCID) [M27], and many videos [M28–M37].

## 2. Main statue (most important)

**Name.** Seated Vairocana Buddha in gilt bronze (銅造盧舎那仏坐像), a National Treasure [S1][S3].

**Posture and mudra.**
- **Posture.** Seated in full lotus (*kekka-fuza*) on a giant lotus throne [S5].
- **Right hand: semui-in** (abhaya, "fear not") [S5][S6]. The forearm is raised from a bent elbow, the hand is at shoulder height in front of the right shoulder, the palm faces the viewer and the fingers point up, slightly spread and relaxed. The middle finger is slightly forward **(uncertain)**.
- **Left hand: yogan-in** (varada, wish-granting) variant [S5][S6]. The arm is lowered and the hand rests on the left knee or thigh with the **palm open and facing up**, fingers pointing forward or slightly up. Some sources call it dhyana; most call it yogan-in [S6].
- The two attendants reverse the hands (left hand raised, right lowered) [S6] **(uncertain for both attendants)**.

**Size.** These are the temple-listed figures [S2][S3].

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Statue height (seated) | **14.98 m** (the Japanese Wikipedia gives about 14.7 m) |
| Face length | 5.33 m |
| Eye width | 1.02 m |
| Nose | 0.50 m (height) |
| Ears | 2.54 m |
| Shoulder span | 28 m (this figure seems large; it may be measured around the curve) **(uncertain)** |
| Hand | The open right hand is "as tall as a person" [S7]. Palm length about 2.5 m, middle finger about 1.1 m **(uncertain)** |
| Weight | about 500 t (bronze), with gold leaf from mercury gilding [S2][S3] |
| Curls (*rahotsu*) | Traditionally 966. A 2015 laser survey counted **492** (plus 9 missing) [S3]. Each is about 20 cm tall **(uncertain)** |
| Lotus pedestal | Upper (upturned) and lower (reflexed) tiers, **28 petals** each (14 large and 14 small, alternating) [S3]. Pedestal circumference about 70 m [S3], so about 22 m across. Each petal is about 2 m high [S8] |
| Halo (*kōhai*) | Gilded wooden halo about **27 m** across (the figure looks large; it may be the full width) **(uncertain)**. It carries **16 small seated Buddhas** (*kebutsu*), each about 2.4 m tall [S2] |

**Proportions in the hall.**
- The hall's inner height to the roof is about 46–48 m [S1][S4].
- The top of the statue's head is about 18 m above the floor: about 3 m of base and lotus plus the 15 m figure **(uncertain)**. The top of the halo is about 27–30 m above the floor **(uncertain)**.
- A 1.7 m adult reaches about the top of the lotus base. The room's 1.1 m avatar comes to about a third of one lotus petal.
- The face alone is about three adults tall.

**Material and surface.**
- Cast bronze, now a **dark, almost black-brown patina** with olive-green and warm-brown variation and glossy highlights on the forehead, nose and knees.
- Only traces of the original gold remain **(uncertain extent)**. Read it as dark bronze, not gold.
- The head (Edo), hands (Momoyama) and body (largely Kamakura and later repairs) have slightly different tones. The original Nara-period parts are the pedestal, the right flank, the hanging sleeves and the thighs [S3].

**Face.**
- Broad, full, slightly square Edo-period face with heavy downcast eyes (half-closed) and long, arched incised brows.
- A small, closed, gently smiling mouth. Very long pierced earlobes.
- A raised round **ūrṇā** (*byakugō*) between the brows, in metal **(uncertain colour; it reads as a bright boss)**.
- A large low ushnisha covered with curls, plus a small round central boss, the *nikkei-shu* **(uncertain)**.
- Three neck folds (*sandō*).

**Robe.**
- A monk's robe with no jewellery, crown or ornaments. Vairocana here is shown as a Buddha, not a crowned bodhisattva.
- The outer robe covers the left shoulder, and its edge is drawn over the right shoulder, so both shoulders look covered. The chest is bare in a deep U or V **(uncertain exact drape)**.
- Shallow, regular, stylized fold ridges run across the chest and sleeves, and heavy sleeves hang over the knees. Robe and body are the same bronze colour.

**Halo.** A huge gilded boat- or flame-shaped wooden aureole behind the whole figure. It is Edo period and was begun after the 1692 head **(uncertain date)**.
- Bright gold leaf with ornate openwork scrolling cloud and flame patterns.
- **16 small gilded Buddhas** set around it [S2].
- It glows warmly behind the dark bronze, and this contrast is the image's signature.

**Throne.**
- A two-tier lotus (upturned and reflexed petals) in bronze, set on a large octagonal stone platform about 2.5 m high **(uncertain height)** with a low railing.
- The original 8th-century petals carry **incised line drawings** of the Lotus Treasury World (*Renge-zō sekai*): a Buddha with bodhisattvas, above horizontal bands of Mount Sumeru cosmology [S1][S3][M13].
- A **full-scale replica petal** is displayed in the hall [M2].

**What surrounds it.**
- **Attendants** (wooden, gilded, about 7 m, Edo period, Important Cultural Properties) [S4][S9]:
  - Viewer's left: **Nyoirin Kannon** (如意輪観音), seated, completed about **1738** [S4].
  - Viewer's right: **Kokūzō Bosatsu** (虚空蔵菩薩), seated, completed **1752** [S4].
  - Both are by the Yamamoto (Junkei) and Tsubai (Kenkei) workshops [S4]. Their side assignment (viewer-left Nyoirin) follows the Commons file names and common guides **(uncertain)**.
  - Both have bright gold surfaces, tall crowns, jewellery and their own gilded halos, which contrast with the dark bronze Buddha.
- **Guardian kings (Shitennō)**: **Kōmokuten** (Virupaksha, སྤྱན་མི་བཟང་།) in the **north-west** corner and **Tamonten** (Vaishravana, རྣམ་ཐོས་སྲས།) in the **north-east** corner. Both are Edo-period, painted wood, about 5 m **(uncertain height)**. Jikokuten and Zōchōten were never finished; only their **heads** are on display, near the back [S4][M10].
- **Altar.** A long front altar (*shumidan* front edge) carries:
  - a pair of large gilt-bronze **flower vases with golden lotus flowers** (*kebyō*), one each side, decorated with **eight-legged butterflies** [S10][M14];
  - a central incense burner and candle stands.
  - A broad wooden **offering box** (*saisen-bako*) stands at floor level in front, where people toss coins and bow.
  - A **candle-offering stand** and an amulet and tile-donation counter stand inside the hall near the entrance **(uncertain position)**.
- There is **no glass case.** The statue is open to view.

### The 5 features that make it instantly recognizable (priority order)
1. **Colossal scale in a dark wooden hall.** A 15 m seated Buddha that fills the view from the doorway, with its head high in the gloom. Scale versus people is the whole impression.
2. **Dark, nearly black bronze body against a huge glowing gold halo** studded with 16 small gold Buddhas.
3. **Right hand raised palm-out (semui-in), left hand resting palm-up on the knee (yogan-in)**, with no crown and a plain monk's robe.
4. **Broad Edo face** with half-closed eyes and long ears, under a dome of very large snail-shell curls (fewer and bigger than usual, at 492).
5. **Giant lotus throne of upright petals** with incised line images, flanked by two **gold seated bodhisattvas** (about 7 m each) and gold lotus vases on the altar.

## 3. Interior

**Dimensions.** The temple's figures: width (E-W) **57.012 m**, depth (N-S) **50.480 m**, height **48.742 m** [S1]. The Japanese Wikipedia gives 46.8 m [S4].
- The building is a single storey with a one-bay pent roof (*mokoshi*) on all sides, so it looks two-storey [S4].
- The facade is 7 bays wide [S1]. Bay widths are about 6–9 m; the centre bay is widest **(uncertain)**.
- The interior is one open volume. **There is no flat ceiling.** You see straight up into the great dark roof structure: tie beams, bracket arms and a dark lattice of timbers.

**Statue position.**
- On the N-S axis, centred or slightly north of centre. The pedestal circle is about 22 m in diameter, centred about 26–28 m north of the south door **(uncertain)**.
- The front of the stone platform is about 13–15 m inside the doorway **(uncertain)**.

**Where devotees stand.**
- **Everyone stands.** There is no seating for visitors and **shoes stay on** **(general knowledge, consistent with photos)**.
- Visitors enter by the **central south doors** and stand in the open floor between the doors and the altar to bow and toss coins.
- They then walk the circuit around the statue: past one attendant, behind the Buddha past the two guardian kings and the pillar with the hole, past the models, and back out by the front.
- Monks hold services on the altar side, in front of the statue. For large ceremonies they set up seats or chairs and lecterns **(uncertain)**.

**Pillars.** The English Wikipedia says **48** pillars, each about 1.5 m in diameter and up to 30 m long [S2].
- They are not single trunks. They are **built-up composite columns** (*yosegi-bashira*): a central log wrapped in planks and **bound with iron hoops**. Two or three hoop bands show on each [S4][M15].
- They are **unpainted dark brown-black aged wood**, matte. The exterior timbers are painted red-brown.
- **"Buddha's Nostril" pillar.** One pillar behind the Buddha, toward the **north-east** **(uncertain corner)**, has a **rectangular hole at its base**, about 37 × 30 cm **(uncertain)**, the size of the Buddha's nostril [S2][M16]. Children crawl through it for good fortune or enlightenment in the next life, and visitors queue to watch.

**Ceiling and upper structure.**
- An open roof structure in dark timber. Big horizontal **penetrating tie beams** (*nuki*) stack between the columns, in the Daibutsuyō (Great Buddha style) structural vocabulary [S4].
- High clerestory openings under the mokoshi let in grey daylight.
- A large **gilded shibi** (ridge-end ornament, kept from a former roof) is displayed on the floor [M17].

**Walls.** White plaster panels between dark posts, with wooden lattice windows high up. There are **no murals**; the interior is austere wood and plaster.

**Floor.** Stone or tile paving in large grey slabs, worn smooth **(uncertain material: stone flags or tiles)**. A gravel courtyard lies outside.

**Lighting.**
- Mostly **daylight through the open south doors**, which are tall: about 8–10 m **(uncertain)**. Light falls on the altar, the lower statue and the lotus, and the face stays in partial shadow.
- Faint grey light comes through the high lattices. There are a few discreet electric spotlights on the statue and the attendants **(uncertain)**.
- Candle light on the offering stand.
- The interior is noticeably dim. The gold halo and the gold attendants catch the light, and the bronze stays dark.

**Banners and textiles.** Long hanging **five-colour silk banners** (*goshiki-maku* curtains or *ban* streamers in blue, yellow, red, white and purple or black) are hung for major ceremonies on the front eaves and in the hall **(uncertain whether they are permanent)**. Hanging gilt lanterns are likely **(uncertain)**.

**Secondary objects.**
- Scale models in the back (north) area: the original Nara-period hall with its two 100 m pagodas, the 12th-century hall, and the 1:50 Nara-era temple layout [M18][S7].
- The full-size lotus-petal replica.
- The heads of Jikokuten and Zōchōten.
- A tile-donation desk where visitors sign roof tiles **(uncertain)**.
- Rope stanchions and wooden information boards.

**Typical clutter.** Coin box, candle racks and lit votives, signs (no flash, no tripods), queue ropes at the nostril pillar, a donation desk, and school groups in uniforms.

## 4. Exterior and surroundings

### The hall
- **Form.** A huge hipped roof (*yosemune*, 寄棟造) with **honkawara-buki** (grey round-and-flat clay tiles) [S4].
  - The single storey plus a **mokoshi** pent roof on all four sides gives two stacked roofs.
  - At the centre of the south facade, a **karahafu** (cusped, wave-shaped gable) bulges over the entrance [S4]. Under it is the **kansō-mado** (観相窓, face-viewing window), a wide horizontal window. It is opened on **1 January and 15 August** so the Buddha's face can be seen from the Chūmon gate [S4][S11].
- **Ridge.** Two large **gold shibi** (fish-tail ridge-end ornaments) stand at the ends of the main ridge.
- **Brackets (Daibutsuyō style).**
  - Many-tiered bracket arms (*sashi-hijiki*) are inserted straight through the pillars.
  - Stacked tie beams (*nuki*) are visible on the exterior.
  - Deep eaves sit on multi-step bracket sets.
  - The style is muscular and "Chinese" (Southern Song derived), not the delicate Japanese wayō [S4][M19].
- **Colours.** Red-brown or vermilion lacquered timbers (posts, beams, brackets), **white plaster** walls, dark grey roofs, gold shibi and gilt metal fittings on the karahafu.
- **Doors and windows.** The front has tall **plank doors with bronze studs** in the central bays. **Lattice windows** (vertical-bar *renji-mado*) and lattice panels run across the upper level.
- **Base.** A low stone podium about 1–1.5 m high with stone steps up to the south doors **(uncertain height)**.

### Courtyard in front of the hall
- **Cloister.** A roofed corridor (*kairō*) with vermilion posts, white walls and lattice windows encloses a large gravel courtyard. It runs from the **Chūmon** (Middle Gate, a two-storey irimoya gate rebuilt 1716 [S4]) to the hall's flanks.
- **Octagonal Lantern** (金銅八角燈籠). A gilt-bronze lantern, a National Treasure from the 8th century, **4.64 m** tall [S1][S4]. Its fire chamber has 8 panels: 4 fixed panels show **music-playing bodhisattvas** (one with a flute), and 4 pairs of doors show **lions running among clouds** [S1][M20]. It stands on the axis directly in front of the hall steps.
- **Great incense burner** (*jōkōro*). A large bronze censer with a roof, on the axis between the lantern and the Chūmon **(uncertain exact position)** [M21]. People light bundles of senkō incense here and waft the smoke over themselves.
- **Chōzuya** (purification basin). A roofed water basin with ladles, about 58 m south of the hall centre and slightly east, inside or at the edge of the courtyard [S19].
- **Binzuru (Pindola).** A red-capped, red-caped wooden seated arhat on the hall's front porch, rubbed by visitors for healing [M22] **(uncertain side)**.
- **Visitor route.** Visitors enter the courtyard by the **west** gate in the corridor (the Chūmon itself stays shut), walk diagonally to the hall, and exit by the **east** side **(general knowledge, uncertain)**.

### Approach and wider precinct
- **Nandaimon** (Great South Gate). A National Treasure from 1199 in Daibutsuyō style: a five-bay, two-storey gate about 25 m tall **(uncertain)** with bare dark-wood brackets [S2][S4].
  - Its two **Niō** guardians, **8.4 m** tall, by Unkei and Kaikei (1203), stand in caged bays facing inward across the passage: Agyō (open mouth) and Ungyō (closed mouth) [S2][S4].
- **Sandō** (approach). A stone-paved avenue about 10 m wide **(uncertain)**, running straight north from the Nandaimon to the Chūmon. Stalls and **deer** crowd it [S7].
- **Kagami-ike** (Mirror Pond). Lies east of the approach, with a small Benzaiten shrine on an island [S4].
- **Bell tower** (*Shōrō*). On a rise to the east (National Treasure, rebuilt 1207–1211) [S4]. It holds the great bell (see section 5).
- **Nigatsu-dō** (Second Month Hall). Up the eastern hillside, on a stilted wooden platform. It hosts the **Shuni-e / Omizutori** rites, 1–14 March [S4].
- **Trees and skyline.** Cherry, maple, pine and cedar. Forested Wakakusa and Kasuga hills rise to the east, with lawns of Nara Park to the south and deer everywhere.

## 5. Sound and instruments

### Chanting

| Group / occasion | What is chanted | Style for synthesis |
|---|---|---|
| **Tōdai-ji daily services (authentic)** | Morning and evening recitation including the **Rishukyō** (Prajñāpāramitā-naya Sūtra) [S4]. **Kegon-kyō** (Avatamsaka) passages and the **Hannya Shingyō** (Heart Sutra) at services and ceremonies [S1][S4] **(Heart Sutra: general practice, uncertain here)**. | Nara and Shingon **shōmyō**: a small group of male voices in unison, low (about 90–150 Hz), slow and melismatic with long held vowels. Sections are paced by the bowl gong (*kin / keisu*) and cymbals, **not** by a wooden fish. |
| **Daibutsu ceremonies** | *Omi-nugui* (statue cleaning) on **7 August**: about 200 monks and laity in white chant sutras before the cleaning [S4][M33]. **Mantō Kuyō-e** (lantern offering) on **15 August**, evening 19:00–22:00, with chanting as lanterns are lit and the face window open [S4][M34]. The Buddha's Birthday **Senzō hōyō** ("thousand-monk service"), about 400 monks [M35]. | Massed unison chant with gongs and cymbals. Gagaku court music (shō, hichiriki, ryūteki, taiko) at grand events **(uncertain at each event)**. |
| **Shuni-e / Omizutori** (Nigatsu-dō, 1–14 March) | Eleven monks (*rengyōshū*) chant the Eleven-Faced Kannon repentance (*keka*), "Nanmu Kanzeon…" [S4] | A fast, accelerating chant. Monks **slam their knees on a wooden board** in prostration (*gotai-tōchi*), there are conch blasts, and wooden clogs stamp on the floor. Dramatic, but held in a different hall. |
| **Pan-East-Asian layer** (optional, not Tōdai-ji) | **Chinese:** morning *zaoke* (Śūraṅgama mantra, Heart Sutra), evening *wanke* (Amitabha Sutra), *nianfo* "Nāmó Āmítuófó". **Korean:** *yebul* (*Chilchŏngnye*, the seven-fold homage), Heart Sutra (*Banya Simgyeong*), *Namu Amita-bul*. **Japanese Pure Land:** *nembutsu* "Namu Amida Butsu". **Vietnamese:** *Nam mô A Di Đà Phật* | **Syllabic and monotone**, about 2–4 syllables a second, **one syllable per wooden-fish stroke**, with the bowl gong at the start and end. Mixed or male voices, about 110–220 Hz. |

### Instruments (what to synthesize)

The *At Tōdai-ji?* column says whether the instrument is documented here, or is pan-East-Asian.

| Instrument | At Tōdai-ji? | When it is played | Sound recipe |

### Ambient
- **Inside the hall:** a large reverberant wooden space with a long, warm tail (RT60 about 4–6 s) **(estimate)**. Crowd murmur in many languages, shoes on stone, coins clinking into the offering box, the occasional guide's voice, and school-group chatter. Hushed at the altar.
- **Courtyard:** gravel footsteps, crows, bulbul and sparrow calls, deer "squeaks" (short high whistles), wind, the hiss of incense smoke, and the clink of the incense-burner lid.
- **Approach:** deer, vendors, and the bell across the hills at night.
- **Seasonal:** cicadas in summer (loud; mid-July to August), rain on tiled roofs.

## 6. Rituals and gestures

### At Tōdai-ji (authentic)
1. **Purify** at the chōzuya: ladle in the right hand, rinse the left hand, then the right, then the mouth from the cupped left palm.
2. **Incense** at the great outdoor burner:
   - Buy a bundle of senkō sticks (often a small fee) and light them at the burner flame.
   - Shake out any flame; never blow on incense.
   - Plant the sticks in the ash.
   - **Waft the smoke over your head and body** with both hands, for health and wisdom.
3. **Bow at the hall threshold.** A short standing bow before entering.
4. **Before the Buddha:**
   - Toss a coin into the saisen box.
   - **Gasshō:** palms and fingers together at the chest, fingertips at nose or chin height, tilted about 15° forward, elbows slightly out.
   - **Bow** from the waist about 30–45°, hold 2–3 s, rise. Optionally a short silent prayer or "Namu Rushana-butsu".
   - **No clapping**; that belongs to Shinto shrines.
5. **Candle offering.** Buy a votive candle, write a wish on it if offered, light it on the stand, and gasshō.
6. **Walk the circuit** around the statue, crawl through the **nostril pillar** (mostly children), rub the **Binzuru** statue on the body part you want healed, and donate a signed roof tile.
7. **Omi-nugui (7 Aug).** Monks in white abseil on ropes down the statue with brooms and dusters after a rite to withdraw the spirit (*hakken*) at 07:00 [S4][M33].

### Circumambulation
Tōdai-ji has no formal circumambulation. Visitors' circuit around the statue usually goes **clockwise**, keeping the Buddha on the right; this matches the Buddhist norm (pradakṣiṇa) **(uncertain whether the hall's actual one-way route is clockwise)**.

### Offerings
- Incense: senkō sticks outside; powdered *shōkō* pinched and dropped on charcoal at services, raised once to the forehead first.
- Candles, coins and flowers (golden metal lotuses on the altar, real flowers at events).
- Signed roof tiles and paper lanterns (at Mantō-e).
- Chinese practice uses **three sticks** held at the forehead, three bows, and the sticks planted in the censer. Korean practice offers **one stick**, or rice and water.

## 9. Build plan

Units are metres. Origin is the centre of the hall's south door threshold at floor level. +X east, +Z south, Y up. The hall faces +Z. The visitor avatar is about 1.1 m tall, so keep the real scale. **The 15 m Buddha towering over a 1.1 m visitor is the point.**

### Must-have
1. **Statue on the axis (priority).**
   - **Platform:** octagonal stone base, radius about 12 m, height 2.5 m, centred at (0, 0, -27). Low stone railing at its edge.
   - **Lotus throne:** bronze, top at y ≈ 5.5. Two tiers of **28 petals** each (alternating large and small). Upper petals about 2 m tall and upright-cupped. Use an incised-line **normal or decal map** on the front petals [M13].
   - **Figure:** seated height **15 m** (y 5.5 → 20.5), full lotus.
     - Right hand raised palm-out at shoulder height (hand about 2.5 m). Left hand palm-up on the left knee.
     - Head about 5.3 m face length, with a dome of **about 490 large curls** (instance them).
     - Long ears, half-closed eyes, ūrṇā boss.
     - Plain robe with shallow fold ridges.
   - **Material:** dark bronze PBR (`#2e2a22`, metalness 0.9, roughness 0.45, slight olive patina variation, brighter worn highlights on the knees and nose).
   - **Halo:** a flat gilded **boat-shaped aureole** about 24–27 m tall behind the figure (y 5 → ~30). Openwork cloud and flame scroll (alpha-tested texture or extruded curves) with **16 small seated gold Buddhas** about 2.4 m each around its edge. Gold PBR (`#c9a24a`, metalness 1, roughness 0.3).
   - **Lighting:** a warm spot (2800 K) from about (0, 30, -5) aimed at the face; a second, softer spot on the hands and lap; a strong cool directional "door shaft" entering from +Z onto the altar and lotus. Low ambient (about 0.04) so the gold halo pops against the dark bronze.
2. **Hall shell.**
   - Interior x ∈ [-28.5, +28.5], z ∈ [-50.5, 0]. Eaves inner height about 20 m at the walls; open roof structure rising to about y = 45.
   - **About 48 composite pillars** (cylinders, r 0.75, dark wood with 2–3 iron hoop rings). Lay them out as a 7-bay front by 6-bay grid **(uncertain layout)**: outer ring plus inner ring, inner ring about 13 m from the walls. Instance them.
   - Stacked horizontal **nuki** beams between the pillars at several heights. Dim open rafters above (texture plus a few beams, mostly in shadow).
   - **South doors:** 3 central openings, each about 6 m wide × 9 m high, at z = 0. Lattice clerestory strips high on all walls emitting faint grey light.
   - Floor: grey stone slabs, y = 0.
3. **Altar zone.**
   - Altar table 10 × 1.2 × 1.1 m at z ≈ -13.
   - Pair of **gilt vases with golden lotus sprays** (about 3 m tall overall) at x = ±5.
   - Central bronze incense burner.
   - Candle stands.
   - **Saisen offering box** 4 × 1 × 0.9 m at (0, 0, -9).
   - This is where visitors bow. Use it for the **incense `burner`** interaction if there is no outdoor one; prefer the outdoor one (item 6).
4. **Attendants.**
   - **Nyoirin Kannon** at (-15, 0, -24) and **Kokūzō** at (+15, 0, -24). About 7 m seated, on lotus pedestals about 3 m tall, so the tops are at about y = 10.
   - All gold, with crowns and their own halos.
   - Stylized but recognizable. Take each pose from the photos (M9 for Nyoirin, M8 for Kokūzō), not from generic iconography: the Tōdai-ji Nyoirin is two-armed, not the usual six-armed form [S9], and its originals were seated with one leg pendant [S9] **(current pose uncertain)**.
5. **Exterior facade** (seen from the courtyard spawn).
   - Hall massing 57 × 50.5 m on a 1.2 m podium.
   - **Two roofs:** a mokoshi skirt roof at y ≈ 14–18, and the main hipped roof from eaves y ≈ 26 up to a ridge at y ≈ 47 with a strong concave curve.
   - Grey tile texture; **gold shibi** at the ridge ends.
   - **Karahafu** cusped gable centred over the doors, with the **kansō-mado** window below it.
   - Vermilion timbers and white plaster; **multi-tier bracket sets** under the eaves (instanced).
   - Studded plank doors.
6. **Courtyard and incense.**
   - Gravel courtyard z ∈ [+4, +100].
   - **Octagonal lantern** 4.64 m at (0, 0, +12).
   - **Great incense burner** (roofed bronze censer, about 2.5 m) at (0, 0, +22). This is the site's `burner`.
   - Spawn at about (0, 0, +45) facing -Z (north), so the whole facade and roofs fill the view.
7. **Bow styles:** `standing-bow` (gasshō) as the default, plus `east-asian-bow` (kneeling prostration).
   - **Seating:** visitors at Tōdai-ji **stand**. For the app's cushions, place a monks' service row: 6–10 flat zabuton or low stools in front of the altar at z ≈ -10 to -11, x from -6 to +6, facing north. ## 10-line summary

1. Temple: **Tōdai-ji Daibutsuden**, Nara (Kegon school, Mahayana). Chosen because interior photography is officially allowed, the main image is one huge central statue, and it is the most famous candidate. Lingyin is the runner-up (photos restricted). Seokguram and Byōdō-in ban photos; Jogyesa and Shaolin have triads. Time zone `Asia/Tokyo`.
2. Hall: 57.0 × 50.5 × 48.7 m wooden hall (1709), a hipped roof plus mokoshi, a karahafu with the face window, gold shibi, Daibutsuyō brackets, about 48 dark composite iron-hooped pillars, no ceiling. Visitors stand, shoes on.
3. Statue feature 1: **colossal scale.** A 14.98 m seated bronze Vairocana on a 28-petal-per-tier lotus about 22 m across. Head about 18 m up, face 5.33 m.
4. Statue feature 2: a **dark, near-black bronze body against a huge gilded halo** with 16 small gold Buddhas.
5. Statue feature 3: **right hand raised palm-out (semui-in), left palm-up on the knee (yogan-in)**, plain robe, no crown.
6. Statue feature 4: a broad Edo face with half-closed eyes, long ears and about 492 very large curls. Feature 5: an incised-petal lotus throne flanked by two gold seated bodhisattvas about 7 m tall (Nyoirin Kannon and Kokūzō), with gold lotus vases on the altar.
7. Grounds: Octagonal Lantern (4.64 m) and great incense burner on the axis, kairō cloister and Chūmon about 100 m south, Nandaimon with 8.4 m Niō about 330 m south, a 26.3 t bell struck by a 4.48 m log, deer everywhere.
8. Practice: gasshō standing bow (default), incense wafted over the body, candles and coins; services use shōmyō with bowl gong and cymbals (Rishukyō, Kegon-kyō). Wooden fish, cloud gong and beopgo are pan-East-Asian layers, not Tōdai-ji.
9. Schedule: hall 07:30–17:30 (Apr–Oct) / 08:00–17:00 (Nov–Mar); Omi-nugui 7 Aug 07:00; Mantō-e 15 Aug 19:00–22:00; New Year bell from 00:00; Shuni-e 1–14 Mar.
10. Open questions: daily gongyō times, whether the bell rings nightly at 20:00, the robe drape, the halo's true size, the exact pillar grid, the attendants' sides and poses, the nostril pillar's position, the incense burner's position, YouTube timestamps. No CC 3D model exists.
