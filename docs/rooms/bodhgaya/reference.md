| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| English | Mahabodhi Temple ("Great Awakening Temple"); officially *Mahabodhi Mahavihara* |
| Hindi (Devanagari) | महाबोधि महाविहार / महाबोधि मंदिर, बोधगया |
| Tibetan | Bodh Gaya is རྡོ་རྗེ་གདན། *Dorje Den* ("Vajra Seat", Skt. Vajrāsana) [S1]. The temple is usually called མ་ཧཱ་བོ་དྷི། *Mahabodhi* **(uncertain: no single standard Tibetan name for the building was found)** |
| Location | Bodh Gaya, Gaya district, Bihar, India. About 24.6959 N, 84.9913 E **(coordinates from general knowledge)** |
| Tradition | **Pan-Buddhist.** Managed by the Bodhgaya Temple Management Committee (BTMC) under the Bodh Gaya Temple Act 1949. Tibetan, Theravada (Sri Lanka, Thailand, Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia), East Asian (Japan, Korea, China, Taiwan, Vietnam), Bhutanese, Nepali, Himalayan and Indian (Ambedkarite) Buddhists all practise on the grounds. |
| Status | UNESCO World Heritage Site no. 1056, inscribed 2002. Core area 4.86 ha [S1][S5] |

**History in brief.**
- Emperor Ashoka marked the site in about the 3rd century BCE, including the polished stone Vajrasana [S1][S9].
- The present brick shikhara temple dates from the Gupta period (5th–6th century CE) [S1].
- Burmese missions restored it in the 13th and 19th centuries.
- Alexander Cunningham and J. D. Beglar restored it heavily in about 1880–84. The sanctum image was reinstated in 1884 [S1].
- Between 2013 and 2014 the top of the spire was gilded with Thai-donated gold, about 289 kg [S1].

**Why this temple.** It is the most sacred site in Buddhism and is very heavily photographed. Wikimedia Commons has 61 interior files, 215 or more exterior files, and dedicated categories for the Vajrasana, the Bodhi Tree, Mucalinda Lake, the Butter Lamp House, the railings and the stupas [S6]. The main image is documented well by its history and inscription [S1][S3]. There are few close, measured descriptions of the sanctum interior, so the sanctum's dimensions in this document are estimates (see section 3).

## 2. Main statue (most important)

**Name.** The Mahabodhi Buddha: Shakyamuni at the moment of Enlightenment (*Māravijaya*, victory over Mara).

**Posture and mudra.**
- **Bhūmisparśa mudrā** (earth-touching), seated in **vajra-paryaṅka** (full lotus) with the right foot on top [S3][S4].
- **Right hand:** the arm hangs over the right knee and the palm faces inward toward the shin. The fingertips touch, or nearly touch, the seat in front of the right knee.
- **Left hand:** rests palm-up in the lap, centred below the navel.
- The figure faces **east**, out through the temple door, as the Buddha is said to have faced at his awakening [S7].

**Date and material.**
- Pala period, 10th–11th century CE [S3].
- Carved in **black chlorite / blackstone** [S3][S4]. It is now fully **covered in gold paint or gilding**. The facial features are painted in **Tibetan style** [S3]. One pilgrim account says Tibetan pilgrims did the gilding [S4].
- Some sources wrongly call it "brass", "bronze" or "yellow sandstone". The stone-with-gilding identification [S3][S4] is the correct one.
- A dedicatory inscription on the pedestal names the donor Sri Purnabhadra of the Chhinda family, with Acharya Jayasena [S3]. Parts of a 13th-century rededication inscription of Pīṭhīpati Jayasena were placed on the plinth in 1884 [S1].

**Size.** Sources disagree:
- About 1.5 m ("over 5 ft") [S5]
- About 2 m [S4, citing Lonely Planet]
- "Over three metres", which probably includes the pedestal [S3]

**Build value:** seated figure about **1.9 m** from seat to top of ushnisha, on a pedestal about **1.0–1.2 m** high. With the backdrop the whole group stands about 3.2–3.5 m. Seated, the figure is about 1.2–1.5 times a standing adult. Viewed from the sanctum floor, its head sits well above eye level.

**Face.**
- Broad, smooth, rounded oval in the Pala manner. The expression is serene with a slight smile [S3].
- Eyes are half-lowered and look down. They are **painted in Tibetan style**: dark pupils, strong dark upper lids and long arched brows [S3]. The exact palette is **(uncertain)**.
- Red lips, a small painted urna, long pierced earlobes.
- Tight snail-shell hair curls with a rounded ushnisha. The hair is painted **dark blue-black (uncertain: blue, as on Tibetan-painted images, is likely; verify in [M3]/[M5])**. A small finial jewel sits on the ushnisha **(uncertain)**.
- Under gilding and lamplight the face reads as **bright, warm gold with crisp dark painted features**. This contrast is the single most recognizable thing about the image.

**Robe and drape cloths.**
- The carved robe is the Pala "wet drapery": the right shoulder is bare and the cloth is so thin it is barely visible.
- In practice the image is almost always **dressed in real cloth robes offered by devotees**. These are **bright orange, saffron, yellow or golden-brown** robes or silk stoles, wrapped over the left shoulder and around the torso, with the right shoulder and right arm left bare so the earth-touching hand shows [S4][S7][S10].
- BTMC monks wrap silk **yellow stoles** around the statue for visiting dignitaries [S10]. Full robe sets are offered at events such as the Kagyu Monlam (three-piece robes of hand-woven golden silk) and the Kathina [S11]. A robe offering takes place **monthly** [S12].
- **Build:** a saffron-orange cloth layer over the gold body. Leave the right shoulder, the right arm, the face and the hands bare gold. The cloth edges should hang in soft folds across the lap and over the pedestal edge.

**Crown and ornaments.**
- Normally **no crown**. This is a monastic Buddha. Photo Dharma's "Crowned Buddha" photo [M24] shows a different image on the site.
- On festival days the image is sometimes garlanded. Small ornaments may be added **(uncertain)**.

**Halo and backdrop (the "arch").**
- The image sits inside a gilded, carved **niche-and-arch backdrop** behind the throne, in the Pala *prabhāvalī* style: a tall rounded or pointed arch with flame or scroll edges. It is set in a deep recess in the sanctum's west wall.
- Exact form, colour and motifs: **(uncertain: check [M3], [M5]–[M8])**.
- **Build:** a gold arch about 2.6 m wide × 3.4 m tall with a raised flame border. Put a round head halo of brighter gold behind the head, and a darker red or deep-gold recess behind that so the gold figure stands out.

**Throne and pedestal.**
- The image sits on a **patterned cushion rather than a double-lotus throne** [S3].
- The plinth has **five niches**: lions in the outer two, elephants in the next two, and the Earth Goddess (Pṛthvī / Paṭhavī) rising in the centre [S3]. She is the witness the Buddha touches the earth to call.
- The plinth is gilded or painted. The inscription runs along it.

**What surrounds it.**
- A **glass screen** protects the image, according to Britannica [S8]. Its exact extent is **(uncertain)**.
- Offerings are banked in front: **lotus flowers (pink and white), marigold garlands, roses**, fruit bowls, water bottles, candles in glass holders and small oil lamps [S13][S14].
- **Resident BTMC monks in saffron** sit to the side and help pilgrims make offerings. A small offering table or ledge with a donation box sits in front **(position uncertain)**.
- The sanctum is lit with warm electric spotlights and lamps. Pilgrims describe "a dark hall with the gold Buddha lit up" [S15].

### The 5 features that make it instantly recognizable (priority order)
1. **A glowing gold earth-touching Buddha in a dark stone chamber, seen straight through the east door.** The right hand reaches down over the right knee and the left hand rests in the lap.
2. **Real saffron or orange robes wrapped over the left shoulder,** with the right shoulder and arm bare gold.
3. **A gold face with crisp, dark, Tibetan-style painted eyes and brows** and dark (blue-black) curled hair with an ushnisha.
4. **A gilded arch-niche backdrop with a round halo,** above a carved plinth with lions, elephants and the Earth Goddess at centre.
5. **A wall of flowers and lights at the base:** pink lotuses, marigolds, candles behind glass, with saffron-robed monks seated at the side.

## 3. Interior

The temple is a two-storey structure [S8][S5]:

```
East door ──► small forecourt (Buddha niches L+R) ──► doorway ──► small hall / vestibule ──► sanctum (statue at W wall)
Upper storey: main hall above the sanctum, with a Buddha shrine, where senior monks meditate. Usually closed to the public.
```

Layout per [S5]: forecourt with Buddha niches on either side, then a doorway, a small hall, and the sanctum. One account says the sanctum is reached "through a narrow passage" [S15].

| Element | Estimate | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Forecourt / porch | 4 m deep × 6 m wide, open to sky or partly roofed | Buddha statues in niches north and south of the door [M16] |
| Doorway | 1.6 m wide × 3.2 m tall, carved stone frame, **(uncertain)** | Shoes are left outside the complex. The floor is bare stone. |
| Vestibule / hall | 3 m wide × 4 m deep × about 5 m high **(uncertain)** | Queue space. Always crowded [S4]. |
| Sanctum | about 6 m wide × 4.5 m deep, ceiling about 7 m **(uncertain: no measured source found; Cunningham 1892 would settle it)** | Statue against the west wall, facing east |
| Statue position | Back of the pedestal against the west wall, centred on the east–west axis | Devotees stand, kneel or prostrate in the 2–3 m in front of it |

**Pillars:** none of note in the sanctum. It is a solid-walled cella. **(uncertain)**

**Ceiling:** high, dark, corbelled or vaulted brick **(uncertain)**. It disappears into shadow.

**Walls:** plain dark stone or plaster, darkened by lamp smoke. There are no large murals. Small framed images, khatas or offered cloths may hang near the image **(uncertain)**.

**Floor:** polished stone or marble, cold underfoot. Devotees kneel on it directly or on small mats.

**Lighting:** very little daylight; it comes only from the east door. Warm spotlights or tube lights are aimed at the image. Candle and oil-lamp flicker at the base. The overall effect is a dark room with one bright gold focus.

**Seating:** none fixed. Pilgrims sit briefly on the floor. Groups chant here only in small numbers because space is tight. Large group chanting happens outside (section 4).

**Secondary statues and clutter:**
- Flower heaps, fruit plates and bottled water.
- Small Buddha images placed by pilgrims.
- A donation box, offered robes folded on the ledge, and incense (though incense is mostly burned outside).

**Upper storey:** a hall above the sanctum with its own Buddha image, used by senior monks for meditation [S5]. The Commons file "First Floor Statue" shows it [M23]. Optional for the room.

**Inner circumambulation:** there is no ambulatory inside. Circumambulation happens outside on three levels (section 4).

## 4. Exterior and surroundings

### The temple

**Form.**
- A straight-sided, truncated-pyramid **shikhara** on a square base. It faces east [S1][S7][S16].
- **Height:** "over 55 m (180 ft)" [S1][S16]. Other sources give 50 m, 54 m, about 170 ft or 48 m. **Build value: 55 m.**
- **Base:** "48 feet square", about **14.6 m** square [S17], plus the eastern porch. **(uncertain: [S2] gives 45 m square, which is almost certainly the whole terrace or enclosure, not the tower)**
- **Lower storey:** walls average **11 m** high [S5]. A terrace or parapet on top carries the **four corner towers**: small shrine chambers, each with a Buddha statue and a miniature shikhara above, copying the main tower [S5][S16].
  - The corner towers are solid [S7].
  - Height **(uncertain)**: about 8–10 m above the terrace, topping out near 20 m from the ground.

**Surface.**
- Fired brick covered in stucco, weathered **grey-tan / buff** [S1][S16].
- Every face carries **tier upon tier of small chaitya-arch niches**, horseshoe-shaped *gavākṣa* motifs [S16], with mouldings between the tiers.
- Above the basement there is a row of niches holding Buddha images [S5]. Wall images show the Buddha in several mudras: abhaya, dharmachakra, bhumisparsha, varada and dhyana. Kirtimukha masks also appear [S13].
- The basement mouldings carry **honeysuckle and geese** motifs [S5].
- Build: 8–10 horizontal bands of repeated niches per face. Each band is about 3–4 m tall. The niches get smaller toward the top.

**East face.** Above the entrance porch is a tall pointed or trefoil **arched opening** that lights the upper hall, running about 1/5 to 1/4 of the tower height **(uncertain: general knowledge, verify in [M15])**.

**Top.**
- The tower ends in a **cylindrical neck** with a ribbed disc (**amalaka**), a pot (**kalasha**), and a **stupa-like finial with a tapering umbrella-mast (chattra)** [S5][S16][S13].
- The top **about 18 ft (5.5 m) is gilded** (2013–14) [S1].

**Night lighting:** the whole tower is floodlit warm yellow-orange against a dark blue sky [M17].

### Sacred spots and the complex

The ensemble sits **about 5 m below the surrounding ground**. Paths and terraces circle the temple and the Lotus Pond on **two or three levels** [S5]. Pilgrims enter from the **east**, then a long flight of steps leads down to a long central paved path toward the temple door [S5].

**Skyline from the courtyard:** the 55 m tower dominates. Nothing else in the town comes close. **Flags and lamps.**
- Tibetan **five-colour prayer flags** (blue, white, red, green, yellow) are strung between trees, railings and posts.

- Strings of marigolds hang everywhere, especially in the festival season.
- Electric lamp posts line the paths. Butter lamps and oil lamps burn in the lamp houses and in lamp racks.

### Ambient
- Many voices murmuring prayers.
- Bare feet on stone.
- Bodhi leaves rustling: high, papery, continuous.
- Birds: mynas, house crows, rose-ringed parakeets, sparrows, pigeons.
- Distant town traffic and horns, muffled because the courtyard is sunken.
- Splashing fish in the pond.
- Crackling butter-lamp flames.
- Occasional portable-speaker chant.
- **Morning:** quiet, birds, and one chant group.
- **Evening:** layered chanting and the PA Pali chant.

## 6. Rituals and gestures

**Bowing styles seen here (pan-Buddhist):**
- **Tibetan / Himalayan full-length prostration:**
  1. Join the palms at the crown, then throat, then heart.
  2. Kneel, then slide forward flat on a **wooden prostration board**, arms stretched.
  3. Stand and repeat, often hundreds of times. Many pilgrims work toward 100,000 [S14][S13][M9].
  - Monks in **dark maroon** robes [S14].
- **Theravada (Sri Lanka, Thailand, Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia):** kneel and do the **five-point bow ×3** (knees, forearms and forehead to the floor), palms in anjali.
  - Sri Lankans wear white and sometimes carry **yellow umbrellas** [S13].
  - Robes: saffron or ochre (Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia); maroon-brown (Myanmar) [S14].
- **East Asian:** standing bow from the waist with gassho / hapjang (palms together), or kneeling full bows. Korean groups may do sets of 108 bows. Robes are **grey or black** [S14].
- **Lay Indian and Ambedkarite Buddhists:** white clothes, standing anjali, floral offerings.
- **Hindu visitors** also come. Some touch the forehead to the railing ("matha tekna") [S13].
- **Nuns:** white or pale pink (Theravada), maroon (Tibetan) [S14].

**Circumambulation:** **clockwise**, keeping the temple on the right, on several concentric levels: the inner path by the railing, the middle terraces, and the outer walkway at street level [S5][S19]. Pilgrims often carry malas or prayer wheels and recite *om mani padme hum* or Pali verses.

**Offerings:**
- Lotuses, marigolds, roses and water lilies [S19].
- Candles and butter lamps (in the lamp houses), incense in the giant burner.
- **Robes / yellow silk stoles for the main image** [S10][S11][S12].
- **Khatas** (white Tibetan scarves; general practice).
- Fruit, water bottles and food.
- Daily **kheer** (rice pudding) at 10:00 [S24].
- Gold foil on the Vajrasana [S9].
- Collecting fallen Bodhi leaves.

## 9. Build plan

Units are metres. Origin is the east door threshold at courtyard level. +X east, +Z south, Y up. The temple faces +X.

### Must-have
1. **Sanctum with the main image** (sections 2 and 3):
   - Room interior x ∈ [-10.5, -4.5], z ∈ [-3, +3], ceiling y = 7.
   - Vestibule x ∈ [-4.5, -0.5], 3 m wide, 5 m high. Door 1.6 × 3.2 m at x = 0.
   - **Pedestal** at x ≈ -9.6: 2.2 w × 1.1 h × 1.4 d, gilded, with 5 relief panels (lion, elephant, Earth Goddess, elephant, lion).
   - **Statue** 1.9 m seated, full lotus, bhumisparsha. Gold PBR material (metalness 0.9, roughness 0.35). Painted face texture with dark eyes and brows and red lips. Dark blue curls. **Saffron cloth robe mesh** over the left shoulder and lap.
   - **Gilded arch** 2.6 × 3.4 m behind, plus a round halo of 0.9 m diameter.
   - **Glass panel** in front at x ≈ -8.6 (thin, transmissive).
   - Flower bank and candles on a ledge at x ≈ -8.3.
   - **Lighting:** 2 warm spotlights (2800 K) from ceiling corners aimed at the face. Dim ambient (about 0.05). Flicker point lights at the candle row. The image must read clearly from the door. **This is the priority.**
2. **Temple tower massing:**
   - Lower block 14.6 × 14.6 × 11 m at x ∈ [-15, 0], z ∈ [-7.3, +7.3].
   - East porch 4 × 6 × 6 m at x ∈ [0, 4].
   - Main shikhara: truncated square pyramid from base 10 m (y = 11) to top 4.5 m (y = 47).
   - Neck cylinder (r 2, y 47–49), amalaka disc (r 2.6, h 1.2, ribbed), kalasha and chattra mast to y = 55.
   - **Gilded material from y ≈ 49.5 up.**
   - **Four corner towers** on the terrace corners: base 3 m, height 9 m (y 11–20), the same profile at small scale.
   - Niche bands: an instanced chaitya-arch niche decal or normal map in about 9 bands per face, with a small Buddha silhouette in each niche on the lower bands.
3. **Bodhi Tree:**
   - Trunk at (-22, 0, 0), trunk diameter about 1.5 m, several leaning limbs.
   - Canopy radius about 12 m, height about 18 m **(uncertain)**.
   - Heart-shaped leaf cards with wind sway. Occasional falling-leaf particle.
4. **Vajrasana** at (-16.6, 0, 0): slab 2.39 × 0.15 × 1.40 on a 0.5 m plinth, under a **gilded throne canopy** (about 2.8 × 1.8 × 1.6 m). Brass railing ring of 3 m radius. Flower and cloth offerings.
5. **Stone railing** (2 m posts, crossbars with lotus medallions, rounded coping) enclosing the temple and tree: rectangle x ∈ [-32, +8], z ∈ [-14, +14], with gaps for the paths.
6. **Sunken courtyard:** courtyard floor y = 0, the surrounding street-level ring at y = +5, retaining walls and stairs. **East steps** x ∈ [+50, +60] (y 5 → 0). Central path 6 m wide from x = +50 to the porch.
7. **Circumambulation path** around the temple (inner, about 3 m wide inside the railing) with clockwise flow for NPCs or avatars.

### Should-have
8. **Chankramana:** platform x ∈ [-14, +2], z = -10.5, 1.1 m wide × 1.0 m high, with **18 carved lotus blossoms** on top at 0.85 m spacing. Row of short pillar bases.
9. **Votive stupa field:** 80–150 instanced stupas, 0.6–4 m, dark grey, in the z ∈ [+14, +40] band and along the path.
10. **Prostration boards:** 3–4 rows of 12 boards each (2.2 × 0.7 × 0.05 m, polished wood) at z ∈ [+16, +24]. Animated prostrating Tibetan figures.
11. **Lotus Pond** centred at (-5, 0, +62), 40 × 30 m, stepped stone edges. Pink lotus pads. **Mucalinda statue** (seated Buddha on the coils with a 7-hooded naga canopy, about 4 m) on a central pedestal.
12. **Animeshlochana Chaitya** at (+30, 3, -35) on a mound: white mini-shikhara about 9 m tall, standing Buddha inside facing the tree (-X).
13. **Butter lamp houses** at about (+15, 0, +48): two glass-fronted sheds, emissive lamp grid. Big incense burner with a smoke particle effect.
14. **Flags:** five-colour prayer-flag strings between trees and railing posts. Buddhist flags on poles. Marigold garlands on the railings.
15. **Monks of several traditions** as sprite or low-poly NPCs: maroon (Tibetan) prostrating and chanting, saffron (Theravada) seated in rows, grey (East Asian) bowing, and white-clad laity.
16. **Audio:** schedule-driven Pali chant at 05:30 and 18:30, Mahayana chant at 18:00, positional Tibetan chant with nga and rolmo, wooden fish, and ambient birds and leaves (section 5). Use the `Asia/Kolkata` clock.

### Nice-to-have
17. Ratnaghar (roofless 4 × 4 m chamber, NE), Ajapala pillar, Rajyatana tree, Buddhapada shrine with black-stone footprints, Ashoka pillar, Mahant memorial.
18. Upper-storey hall with an arched east window and a Buddha image.
19. Day/night cycle: warm floodlit tower at night, glowing butter lamps.
20. Festival modes: Tipitaka chant in December (crowds under the tree), Monlam with dungchen.
21. Meditation Park (SE), the outer street-level walkway with shops, and a distant skyline of monastery roofs.

### Free 3D models and textures
- **No freely licensed (CC0 / CC-BY) 3D model of the Mahabodhi Temple or its statue was found.** Build procedurally. The Rubin Museum stone model photos [M38] are a good massing guide.
- **CC0 textures:** ambientCG (https://ambientcg.com) and Poly Haven (https://polyhaven.com/textures). Use "Bricks", "PavingStones", "Marble" and "Plaster" for the tower stucco, courtyard paving and marble, plus a weathered sandstone texture for the railings and stupas. Gold comes from the PBR material parameters.
- **CC0 HDRI:** Poly Haven outdoor HDRIs, for example a hazy clear afternoon sky, for daylight image-based lighting.
- **Bodhi Tree:** generate procedurally (for example with a three.js tree generator), or search Sketchfab for a "Ficus religiosa / peepal" model with a CC-BY filter. **(none verified)**
- **Statue:** hand-model or sculpt. Earth-touching Buddha scans on Sketchfab (CC-BY) could serve as a base mesh, with Pala proportions and the robe added **(none verified)**.

## 10-line summary

1. Temple: Mahabodhi Mahavihara, Bodh Gaya, Bihar (UNESCO 1056). Pan-Buddhist, run by the BTMC. Time zone `Asia/Kolkata`. Open 05:00–21:00, chanting 05:30, 18:00 (Mahayana) and 18:30 (Pali).
2. Exterior: 55 m straight-sided brick shikhara on a 14.6 m square base, four corner towers, niche bands, gilded top 5.5 m, in a sunken courtyard 5 m deep entered from the east.
3. Behind the temple (west): the Vajrasana slab (2.39 × 1.40 m) under a gilded canopy, then the Bodhi Tree, both inside 2 m Sunga/Gupta-style stone railings.
4. Statue feature 1: a glowing gold earth-touching Buddha (Pala, 10th–11th century, black stone, gilded), seen through the dark east doorway.
5. Statue feature 2: real saffron or orange offered robes over the left shoulder; the right shoulder and arm are bare gold.
6. Statue feature 3: a gold face with dark Tibetan-style painted eyes and brows, and dark (probably blue-black) curls and ushnisha.
7. Statue feature 4: a gilded arch-niche and halo above a plinth carved with lions, elephants and the Earth Goddess. Feature 5: a bank of lotuses, marigolds and candles behind glass, with saffron monks at the side.
8. Grounds: Jewel Walk with 18 lotuses on the north side, Animeshlochana NE, Ratnaghar NE, Mucalinda pond with a naga statue to the south, butter lamp houses, votive stupas, prostration boards, prayer flags.
9. Practice: Tibetan full prostrations, Theravada five-point bows and Pali chant, East Asian standing bows with the wooden fish, clockwise circumambulation on 2–3 levels.
10. Open questions: measured sanctum size and ceiling height, the exact backdrop and arch design, hair colour, the glass extent, corner-tower height, Bodhi Tree size, Jewel Walk length, prostration-board positions, YouTube timestamps. No CC 3D model exists.
